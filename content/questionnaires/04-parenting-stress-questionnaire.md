## A Note Before You Begin

Every parent feels stressed sometimes. This questionnaire helps you understand WHERE the stress lives so you can address it. This is not about being a "good" or "bad" parent — it's about taking care of the person doing the parenting: YOU.

## Part 3: Stress Reduction Strategies

### For Personal Distress

**Identity:**
- Schedule 30 minutes weekly for something that's JUST for you (not parenting or work)
- Remind yourself: "I am [your name] first, and a parent second"
- Reconnect with a pre-parenting hobby

**Guilt:**
- Replace "I should" with "I'm doing my best with what I have"
- Good enough parenting IS good parenting
- Your child needs a present, imperfect parent — not a perfect, exhausted one

**Isolation:**
- Join a parent support group (online or offline)
- Schedule regular meetups with friends (even 30 minutes)
- Talk to other parents honestly — they're struggling too

### For Parent-Child Interaction Stress

**Patience:**
- When patience runs out, take a physical pause (leave the room for 60 seconds)
- Name your emotion aloud: "Mummy is feeling frustrated right now. I need a minute."
- Lower your standards on hard days — survival mode is okay

**Connection:**
- 10 minutes of undivided attention daily (follow your child's lead)
- Replace "How was school?" with "What was the best part of your day?"
- Physical affection: hugs, high-fives, hand-holding

**Discipline:**
- State what you WANT, not just what you don't want
- Offer choices: "Do you want to do homework now or after a snack?"
- Natural consequences > punishment

### For External Pressures

**Partner disagreements:**
- Agree on 3–5 core parenting values together
- Present a united front to your child; discuss differences privately
- Consider couples counselling if parenting conflicts are ongoing

**Family interference:**
- "Thank you for your concern. We've decided to handle it this way."
- Set information boundaries — not everything needs to be shared
- Remember: your child, your rules (with respect, not rebellion)

## The "Good Enough Parent" Manifesto

- I don't need to be perfect. I need to be present.
- My child needs my love, not my martyrdom.
- Saying "I'm sorry" to my child teaches them strength, not weakness.
- Rest is not optional — it's part of being a good parent.
- I'm allowed to ask for help.
- My feelings matter too.
- I'm doing better than I think I am.
