# New Parent Mental Health — Self-Check (Both Partners)
### Ms Paul Therapies | @mspaultherapies

> 📱 @mspaultherapies | 🌐 mspaultherapies.com | 📧 mspaultherapies@gmail.com

**Note:** Paternal postnatal depression is real too. This checklist is for ALL new parents.

---

## Check all that apply (past 2 weeks):

### Emotional
- ☐ Feeling overwhelmed by the responsibility
- ☐ Guilt about not enjoying parenthood enough
- ☐ Resentment toward your partner or the baby
- ☐ Grief for your pre-baby life or identity
- ☐ Irritability or anger outbursts
- ☐ Anxiety about the baby's health, safety, or development
- ☐ Feeling like a failure as a parent
- ☐ Emotional numbness or disconnection

### Relationship
- ☐ Feeling unsupported by your partner
- ☐ Arguments increasing since the baby arrived
- ☐ Intimacy (emotional or physical) has disappeared
- ☐ Feeling like you're parenting alone
- ☐ Competing over who is more tired or doing more

### Physical
- ☐ Severe sleep deprivation affecting functioning
- ☐ Neglecting your own health (meals, hygiene, exercise)
- ☐ Physical recovery issues (for birthing parent)
- ☐ Feeling touched-out or overstimulated

### Social
- ☐ Feeling isolated from friends
- ☐ Losing your identity outside of "parent"
- ☐ Comparison with other parents on social media
- ☐ Not asking for help even when you need it

## Scoring

| Checked | Level | Action |
|---------|-------|--------|
| 0–4 | Normal adjustment | Self-care + accept help |
| 5–10 | Elevated distress | Talk to partner/friend + consider therapy |
| 11–16 | Significant | Professional support recommended |
| 16+ | Urgent | Seek help immediately |

## What New Parents Need to Hear

- You don't have to enjoy every moment to be a good parent
- Asking for help is responsible, not weak
- Your mental health matters for your baby's wellbeing too
- The transition to parenthood is one of life's hardest adjustments
