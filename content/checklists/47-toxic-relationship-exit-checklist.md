# Leaving a Toxic Relationship — Safety & Recovery Checklist

## Before Leaving — Safety Planning

- ☐ Tell at least one trusted person about your plan
- ☐ Save important documents (ID, passport, bank records) in a safe place
- ☐ Set aside emergency money (cash or separate account)
- ☐ Identify a safe place to go (friend, family, shelter)
- ☐ Save helpline numbers in your phone under a different name
- ☐ Document any abuse (screenshots, photos, journal entries)
- ☐ Plan for children and pets if applicable
- ☐ Clear your browser history if your partner checks your devices

## During the Transition

- ☐ Remove yourself from shared devices and accounts
- ☐ Change passwords on email, banking, social media
- ☐ Block or restrict contact if needed
- ☐ Inform close friends/family for support
- ☐ Seek legal advice if married or cohabiting
- ☐ Allow yourself to grieve — leaving is a loss, even from a bad relationship

## Emotional Recovery (The Hard Part)

- ☐ Expect grief, relief, guilt, anger, and doubt — sometimes all in one day
- ☐ Resist the urge to go back during weak moments (write yourself a letter NOW about why you left)
- ☐ Avoid contact with your ex during the initial recovery period
- ☐ Don't rush into a new relationship
- ☐ Rebuild self-esteem through therapy, journaling, and self-care
- ☐ Reconnect with people and activities the relationship took from you
- ☐ Be patient — recovery takes time, and healing isn't linear

## You Are Not to Blame

Staying in a toxic relationship doesn't make you weak. Leaving one makes you incredibly brave. Your reasons for staying were valid at the time. Your choice to leave is valid now.
