## Understanding the Difference

**Guilt** = "I did something bad" (about behaviour — can be productive)
**Shame** = "I AM bad" (about identity — almost always destructive)

## Shame Patterns — Check all that apply:

- ☐ Persistent feeling of being fundamentally flawed
- ☐ Fear of being "found out" (imposter feelings tied to worth)
- ☐ Hiding parts of yourself from others
- ☐ Believing you don't deserve good things
- ☐ Shrinking yourself to avoid attention or judgement
- ☐ Difficulty accepting compliments or love
- ☐ Perfectionism driven by fear of being seen as inadequate
- ☐ Avoiding vulnerability at all costs
- ☐ Inner voice that says "You're not enough"
- ☐ Shame about your body, identity, family, background, or past

## Guilt Patterns — Check all that apply:

- ☐ Feeling guilty for prioritising yourself
- ☐ Guilt about setting boundaries
- ☐ Carrying guilt for things that weren't your fault
- ☐ Apologising excessively
- ☐ Guilt about not meeting family expectations
- ☐ Feeling guilty for being happy when others aren't
- ☐ Guilt about past mistakes you've already addressed
- ☐ Parental guilt (not doing enough for your kids)
- ☐ Survivor's guilt (doing better than peers, siblings)
- ☐ Cultural guilt ("I should be more traditional/obedient")

## Scoring

**Shame items 6+:** Shame is significantly affecting your self-worth. Therapy (especially shame-focused or self-compassion work) is strongly recommended.

**Guilt items 6+:** Unhealthy guilt patterns are draining your energy. CBT and values clarification can help you distinguish productive guilt from toxic guilt.

## First Steps

1. Name it: "This is shame/guilt. It's a feeling, not a fact."
2. Share it: Shame cannot survive being spoken to someone safe
3. Challenge it: "Is this guilt helping me grow or just hurting me?"
4. Self-compassion: Treat yourself as you would treat a dear friend
