## Where Are You in the Healing Process?

### Acute Phase (First 0–6 months)
- ☐ Shock, disbelief, or numbness
- ☐ Intense grief — crying frequently
- ☐ Anger toward your ex-spouse
- ☐ Relief mixed with guilt
- ☐ Difficulty with daily functioning
- ☐ Sleep and appetite disruptions
- ☐ Feeling like a failure
- ☐ Anxiety about the future (finances, children, being alone)
- ☐ Shame — especially in Indian social context

### Processing Phase (6–18 months)
- ☐ Beginning to understand what went wrong
- ☐ Grieving the future you imagined
- ☐ Navigating co-parenting challenges (if applicable)
- ☐ Rebuilding daily routines
- ☐ Handling others' opinions and judgements
- ☐ Loneliness — especially evenings and weekends
- ☐ Financial stress and adjustment
- ☐ Identity rebuilding ("Who am I now?")

### Growth Phase (18+ months)
- ☐ Accepting what happened without bitterness
- ☐ Rediscovering yourself and your interests
- ☐ Healthy boundaries with your ex
- ☐ Openness to new relationships (without rushing)
- ☐ Reduced emotional charge when thinking about the marriage
- ☐ Gratitude for lessons learned
- ☐ Future feels possible, even exciting

## Healing Is Not Linear

You might feel "Growth Phase" one week and "Acute Phase" the next. That's normal. Healing is messy, and triggers (anniversaries, mutual friends, social media) can reactivate grief.

## When to Seek Help

Therapy is beneficial at ANY phase. It can help you:
- Process grief and anger safely
- Navigate co-parenting with less conflict
- Rebuild self-esteem and identity
- Avoid repeating patterns in future relationships
- Manage the social stigma of divorce in India
