# Eating Disorder Warning Signs — Awareness Checklist
### Ms Paul Therapies | @mspaultherapies

> 📱 @mspaultherapies | 🌐 mspaultherapies.com | 📧 mspaultherapies@gmail.com

**For:** Individuals, parents, friends, and partners. Early detection saves lives.

---

## Behavioural Signs
- ☐ Restricting food intake or skipping meals regularly
- ☐ Eliminating entire food groups without medical reason
- ☐ Eating large amounts of food in a short period (bingeing)
- ☐ Going to the bathroom immediately after meals
- ☐ Excessive exercise (even when injured, ill, or exhausted)
- ☐ Hiding food or eating in secret
- ☐ Counting calories, weighing food, or tracking obsessively
- ☐ Chewing food and spitting it out
- ☐ Using laxatives, diet pills, or purging methods
- ☐ Cooking elaborate meals for others but not eating them

## Psychological Signs
- ☐ Intense fear of gaining weight
- ☐ Body image distortion ("I'm fat" when objectively not)
- ☐ Self-worth heavily tied to weight or appearance
- ☐ Guilt or shame after eating
- ☐ Obsessive thoughts about food, weight, or body shape
- ☐ Social withdrawal (especially from food-related events)
- ☐ Mood swings, irritability, or depression related to eating
- ☐ Black-and-white thinking about food ("good" vs "bad" foods)
- ☐ Perfectionism and need for control

## Physical Signs
- ☐ Significant weight loss or fluctuations
- ☐ Feeling cold all the time
- ☐ Dizziness or fainting
- ☐ Hair loss or brittle nails
- ☐ Irregular or absent menstrual periods
- ☐ Dental problems (erosion from purging)
- ☐ Gastrointestinal issues (bloating, constipation)
- ☐ Fatigue and weakness
- ☐ Swelling around jaw or cheeks (purging)

## Indian Context
- Diet culture disguised as "health" (keto, intermittent fasting taken to extremes)
- Family comments about weight normalised ("You've become so fat/thin")
- Marriage pressure ("Lose weight before the wedding")
- Comparison with siblings, cousins, or peers
- Festival/food culture creating guilt cycles

## If You Recognise 5+ Signs

Please reach out to a mental health professional. Eating disorders are serious but treatable with the right support — therapy, nutrition counselling, and sometimes medical care.
