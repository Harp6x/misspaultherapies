# Bipolar Mood Monitoring — Awareness Checklist

> 📱 @mspaultherapies | 🌐 mspaultherapies.com | 📧 mspaultherapies@gmail.com

**Important:** Bipolar disorder requires psychiatric diagnosis and often medication. This checklist is for awareness only — not self-diagnosis.

---

## Part A: Have You Experienced a "High" Period? (Mania/Hypomania)

A period lasting at least 4 days where you felt noticeably different from your usual self:

- ☐ Feeling unusually happy, energetic, or "on top of the world"
- ☐ Needing significantly less sleep (e.g., 3–4 hours) without feeling tired
- ☐ Talking much more than usual or faster than normal
- ☐ Racing thoughts — ideas coming too fast to keep up
- ☐ Being easily distracted — jumping between activities
- ☐ Increased goal-directed activity (starting many projects, cleaning at 3 AM)
- ☐ Impulsive spending, risky decisions, or uncharacteristic behaviour
- ☐ Increased confidence or grandiosity ("I can do anything")
- ☐ Increased sociability or inappropriate social behaviour
- ☐ Irritability (feeling wired, agitated, easily angered)

## Part B: Have You Experienced "Low" Periods? (Depression)

- ☐ Persistent sadness or emptiness for 2+ weeks
- ☐ Loss of interest in things you normally enjoy
- ☐ Sleeping too much or too little
- ☐ Significant weight/appetite changes
- ☐ Fatigue or loss of energy
- ☐ Difficulty concentrating or making decisions
- ☐ Feelings of worthlessness or excessive guilt
- ☐ Thoughts of death or self-harm

## Part C: Pattern Recognition

- ☐ You cycle between Parts A and B
- ☐ These episodes are clearly different from your "normal" self
- ☐ Episodes last days to weeks (not just hours)
- ☐ Family history of bipolar disorder or mood disorders
- ☐ Antidepressants alone made you feel worse or triggered a "high"
- ☐ Others have noticed these shifts in you

## When to Seek Help

If you checked 4+ items in Part A AND 4+ in Part B, especially with patterns from Part C, please consult a psychiatrist for proper evaluation. Bipolar disorder is highly treatable with the right combination of medication and therapy.

## What You Can Do Now

1. **Track your moods daily** using our Mood Tracking Checklist
2. **Maintain a regular sleep schedule** — sleep disruption is a key trigger
3. **Avoid alcohol and recreational substances** — they destabilise mood
4. **Tell someone you trust** about what you're experiencing
