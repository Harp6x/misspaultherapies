## Do You Get Overwhelmed By Sensory Input?

- ☐ Loud environments (restaurants, markets, traffic) drain you quickly
- ☐ Certain textures (clothing tags, fabrics) feel unbearable
- ☐ Bright or fluorescent lights bother you
- ☐ Strong smells trigger headaches or nausea
- ☐ Multiple conversations at once feel impossible to process
- ☐ Crowds make you anxious or irritable
- ☐ You need quiet time after social events to recover
- ☐ Background noise (TV, music) while concentrating is distressing
- ☐ You startle easily at sudden sounds
- ☐ Certain foods' textures are intolerable
- ☐ You feel "touched out" after physical contact
- ☐ You notice details others miss (humming appliances, flickering lights)
- ☐ Transitions between environments (quiet to loud) are jarring
- ☐ You need to control your environment to feel calm

## When Overwhelm Hits — Emergency Kit

1. ☐ Remove yourself from the stimulus if possible
2. ☐ Noise-cancelling earbuds or headphones
3. ☐ Sunglasses for light sensitivity
4. ☐ Deep pressure (weighted blanket, tight hug, press palms together)
5. ☐ Cold water on wrists or face
6. ☐ Focus on one sense at a time (close eyes, focus on breathing)
7. ☐ Stim/fidget tool (stress ball, textured ring)
8. ☐ 5 minutes alone in a quiet space

## Long-Term Strategies

- ☐ Plan social events with exit strategies
- ☐ Carry a sensory kit (earbuds, sunglasses, fidget, gum/mint)
- ☐ Schedule decompression time after high-stimulation activities
- ☐ Communicate needs to loved ones without shame
- ☐ Design your living/work space for sensory comfort
- ☐ Limit screen time during high-sensitivity days
