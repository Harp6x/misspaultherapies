## Part 1: Signs You May Benefit from Therapy

Check any that apply to you:

### Emotional Signs
- [ ] I feel sad, anxious, or angry most of the time
- [ ] My emotions feel overwhelming or out of control
- [ ] I feel emotionally numb or disconnected
- [ ] I cry more often than usual
- [ ] I feel hopeless about the future
- [ ] I feel intense guilt or shame regularly

### Behavioural Signs
- [ ] I've been withdrawing from people I care about
- [ ] I'm using alcohol, food, shopping, or screens to cope
- [ ] My sleep pattern has significantly changed
- [ ] I've lost interest in things I used to enjoy
- [ ] I procrastinate or avoid important tasks
- [ ] I've been snapping at people or feeling easily irritated

### Relationship Signs
- [ ] I'm having ongoing conflicts with my partner, family, or friends
- [ ] I feel misunderstood or unsupported by the people around me
- [ ] I struggle to set boundaries or say no
- [ ] I keep repeating unhealthy relationship patterns
- [ ] I feel lonely even when surrounded by people

### Life Situation Signs
- [ ] I'm going through a major life change (move, job change, loss, marriage, baby)
- [ ] I've experienced a traumatic event (recent or past)
- [ ] I'm dealing with a health diagnosis
- [ ] I feel stuck in my career or life direction
- [ ] I'm struggling with cultural or identity conflicts

### Duration Signs
- [ ] These issues have lasted more than 2 weeks
- [ ] Self-help strategies aren't enough anymore
- [ ] Friends and family can't provide the support I need
- [ ] I've been thinking "maybe I should talk to someone"

**If you checked 5 or more items, therapy could be genuinely helpful for you.**

## Part 2: Common Concerns About Starting Therapy

| Concern | The Reality |
|---------|------------|
| "I'm not 'crazy' enough for therapy" | Therapy is for anyone who wants to feel better, not just those in crisis |
| "I should handle this on my own" | Strength is knowing when to get support, not suffering in silence |
| "Therapy is only for Westerners" | Mental health is universal. Indian culture also values wisdom-seeking |
| "What if people find out?" | Therapy is confidential. Your therapist cannot share without your consent |
| "I can't afford it" | Many therapists offer sliding scale. Online therapy is often more affordable |
| "What will my family think?" | You can keep therapy private. Your growth will speak for itself |
| "It won't work for me" | Research shows therapy helps 75% of people who try it |
| "I don't know what to talk about" | Your therapist will guide you. You don't need a script |

## Part 3: What to Look for in a Therapist

### Essential Qualities
- [ ] Licensed/registered (RCI registration for India)
- [ ] Trained in evidence-based approaches (CBT, DBT, psychodynamic, etc.)
- [ ] You feel safe and un-judged with them
- [ ] They respect your cultural background
- [ ] They explain their approach clearly
- [ ] You feel heard (not lectured)

### Good Questions to Ask a Therapist
1. "What is your therapeutic approach?"
2. "Have you worked with issues like mine before?"
3. "How do sessions typically work?"
4. "What does confidentiality look like?"
5. "How will we know if therapy is working?"
6. "What are your fees and is there a sliding scale?"

### Red Flags
- [ ] They tell you what to do instead of helping you discover it
- [ ] They share too much about their own life
- [ ] They dismiss your cultural context
- [ ] They don't explain confidentiality clearly
- [ ] You feel worse after every session (not just sometimes — consistently)
- [ ] They discourage you from seeking a second opinion

## Part 4: Preparing for Your First Session

### What to Expect
- First sessions are usually about getting to know each other
- You'll be asked about your history, current concerns, and goals
- You set the pace — you don't have to share everything at once
- It's normal to feel nervous
- It's okay to cry, be silent, or not have the right words

### What to Bring (Mentally)
- [ ] A general idea of what you want help with
- [ ] Any questions you have about the process
- [ ] An open mind — therapy may surprise you
- [ ] Self-compassion — showing up is the hardest part, and you've done it
