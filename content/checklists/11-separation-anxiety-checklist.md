## Check all that apply (past 4 weeks):

### Thoughts & Fears
- ☐ Excessive worry about losing a loved one (accident, illness, death)
- ☐ Persistent fear that something bad will happen when apart from attachment figure
- ☐ Worry that an event (kidnapping, getting lost, accident) will lead to permanent separation
- ☐ Reluctance to go out alone due to fear of separation
- ☐ Nightmares about separation themes
- ☐ Constant need to know where your loved one is

### Behaviours
- ☐ Reluctance or refusal to go to work/school/college without the person
- ☐ Difficulty being alone at home
- ☐ Calling/texting loved ones excessively when apart
- ☐ Following a partner or parent around the house
- ☐ Needing someone present to fall asleep
- ☐ Avoiding travel, trips, or outings that require separation

### Physical Symptoms (When Separated or Anticipating Separation)
- ☐ Headaches
- ☐ Stomach aches or nausea
- ☐ Racing heart or chest tightness
- ☐ Crying or emotional outbursts
- ☐ Panic attacks

### Impact
- ☐ Missing work, college, or social events
- ☐ Relationships strained by clinginess
- ☐ Unable to function independently
- ☐ Distress lasts beyond what's expected for your age

## Indian Context Considerations

Separation anxiety in Indian families can be normalised or masked:
- Moving out for college/work may trigger intense anxiety dismissed as "missing home"
- Marriage and moving to a new family can trigger separation responses
- Parents may experience separation anxiety when children become independent
- It's common in joint family structures transitioning to nuclear setups

## When to Seek Help

If 8+ items are checked and symptoms persist for 4+ weeks, professional support is recommended. Separation anxiety in adults is more common than people realise and responds well to therapy.
