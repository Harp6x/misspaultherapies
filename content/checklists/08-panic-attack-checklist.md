## Part 1: Am I Having a Panic Attack?

A panic attack is a sudden surge of intense fear or discomfort that peaks within minutes. Check symptoms you're experiencing:

### Physical
- ☐ Racing or pounding heart
- ☐ Chest pain or tightness
- ☐ Shortness of breath or feeling smothered
- ☐ Dizziness or lightheadedness
- ☐ Tingling or numbness in hands/feet
- ☐ Hot flashes or chills
- ☐ Sweating
- ☐ Nausea or stomach distress
- ☐ Trembling or shaking
- ☐ Feeling unsteady or faint

### Psychological
- ☐ Fear of dying
- ☐ Fear of losing control or "going crazy"
- ☐ Feeling detached from your body (depersonalisation)
- ☐ Feeling the world is unreal (derealisation)
- ☐ Overwhelming urge to escape

**If 4+ symptoms appear suddenly and peak within 10 minutes → likely a panic attack.**

> **Important:** If you've never had a panic attack before and have chest pain, please rule out cardiac issues with a doctor first.

## Part 2: During a Panic Attack — Grounding Protocol

Follow these steps IN ORDER:

### Step 1: Remind Yourself
☐ "This is a panic attack. It is not dangerous. It will pass."
☐ "My body is in fight-or-flight mode. This is uncomfortable but not harmful."

### Step 2: Slow Your Breathing
☐ Inhale through nose for 4 counts
☐ Hold for 4 counts
☐ Exhale through mouth for 6 counts
☐ Repeat 5 times

### Step 3: Ground with 5-4-3-2-1
☐ Name 5 things you can SEE
☐ Name 4 things you can TOUCH
☐ Name 3 things you can HEAR
☐ Name 2 things you can SMELL
☐ Name 1 thing you can TASTE

### Step 4: Body Release
☐ Unclench your jaw
☐ Drop your shoulders away from your ears
☐ Unfist your hands
☐ Place feet flat on the ground

### Step 5: Ride It Out
☐ Stay where you are if safe (running reinforces fear)
☐ Remind yourself: "Peak is 10 minutes. I can do 10 minutes."

## Part 3: After the Panic Attack

- ☐ Drink water slowly
- ☐ Take 5 minutes of rest
- ☐ Journal what happened (trigger, symptoms, duration, what helped)
- ☐ Be gentle with yourself — panic attacks are exhausting
- ☐ Do NOT avoid the place/situation (this builds more fear long-term)
