## What Is Burnout?

Burnout is not just being tired. It is a state of chronic physical and emotional exhaustion caused by prolonged stress — usually related to work, caregiving, or academic demands.

The World Health Organization recognises burnout as an "occupational phenomenon" with three dimensions:
1. **Exhaustion** — feeling drained and depleted
2. **Cynicism** — feeling detached, negative, or indifferent
3. **Reduced efficacy** — feeling incompetent or unproductive

## Part 3: Physical Warning Signs Checklist

Burnout manifests in the body. Check any you're experiencing:

- [ ] Frequent headaches or migraines
- [ ] Jaw clenching or teeth grinding
- [ ] Neck, shoulder, or back tension
- [ ] Digestive issues (IBS, acid reflux, nausea)
- [ ] Frequent colds or infections
- [ ] Hair loss
- [ ] Skin breakouts or flare-ups
- [ ] Heart palpitations
- [ ] Changes in appetite (eating too much or too little)
- [ ] Insomnia or hypersomnia

**If you checked 4 or more, your body is signalling distress.**

## Part 4: The Burnout Recovery Plan

### Immediate Actions (This Week)
- [ ] Take at least one full day off (no work, no productivity pressure)
- [ ] Set one firm boundary at work (leave on time, say no to one request)
- [ ] Sleep 8 hours for 3 consecutive nights
- [ ] Move your body for 20 minutes daily (walk, stretch, dance)
- [ ] Tell one person you trust how you're feeling

### Short-Term (This Month)
- [ ] Identify your top 3 stressors and make a plan for each
- [ ] Reduce commitments — drop one non-essential obligation
- [ ] Schedule something enjoyable that has nothing to do with work
- [ ] Limit after-hours work communication
- [ ] Consider speaking to a therapist or counsellor

### Long-Term (Ongoing)
- [ ] Re-evaluate workload and negotiate if needed
- [ ] Build a regular exercise routine
- [ ] Develop a consistent sleep schedule
- [ ] Cultivate relationships outside of work
- [ ] Reconnect with hobbies and interests
- [ ] Consider whether your current role aligns with your values

## Part 5: The Indian Professional Context

Burnout in India has unique drivers:

| Factor | Impact |
|--------|--------|
| "Hustle culture" glorification | Makes rest feel like failure |
| Long commutes | Drains energy before work even starts |
| Family expectations | Pressure to earn, succeed, and care for elders |
| Limited work-life boundaries | Boss messaging at 10pm feels normal |
| Stigma around saying "I'm struggling" | People suffer silently |
| Competitive environment since school | Worth tied to achievement |

**Permission reminder:** You are allowed to:
- Be tired without feeling guilty
- Set limits without feeling selfish
- Ask for help without feeling weak
- Choose rest over hustle
- Say "this is too much"
