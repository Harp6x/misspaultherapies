## What Are Boundaries?

Boundaries are the limits you set about what is acceptable in how others treat you and how you treat yourself. They protect your energy, time, emotions, and well-being.

**Boundaries are not:**
- Selfish
- Rude
- A rejection of someone
- A sign you don't care

**Boundaries are:**
- An act of self-respect
- Necessary for healthy relationships
- A way of communicating your needs
- Essential for mental health

## Part 1: Boundary Assessment

### Where Are Your Boundaries Weak?

Check any that sound like you:

#### With Family
- [ ] I say yes to family demands even when I'm exhausted
- [ ] I feel responsible for my parents' emotions
- [ ] I can't disagree without feeling guilty
- [ ] Family members make decisions for me
- [ ] I tolerate comments about my body, marriage, career, etc.
- [ ] My privacy is not respected (room, phone, relationships)

#### At Work
- [ ] I work after hours regularly without compensation
- [ ] I take on other people's tasks to avoid conflict
- [ ] I can't say no to my boss even when overloaded
- [ ] I check work messages during personal time compulsively
- [ ] I feel responsible for my colleagues' wellbeing or mistakes
- [ ] I skip lunch, breaks, or leave to appear dedicated

#### In Friendships
- [ ] I always listen to others' problems but never share mine
- [ ] I feel drained after spending time with certain friends
- [ ] I agree with opinions I don't hold to avoid conflict
- [ ] I feel obligated to respond to messages immediately
- [ ] I lend money or resources I can't afford to give

#### With Yourself
- [ ] I push through exhaustion instead of resting
- [ ] I engage in negative self-talk
- [ ] I break promises to myself (exercise, sleep, hobbies)
- [ ] I scroll social media even when it makes me feel bad
- [ ] I neglect my own needs to care for others

**Count your checks: ___**
- **0–5:** Your boundaries are relatively healthy
- **6–12:** Some areas need attention
- **13+:** Boundary work should be a priority

## Part 2: Types of Boundaries

| Type | What It Protects | Example |
|------|-----------------|---------|
| **Physical** | Body, space, touch | "I'm not comfortable with hugs from people I don't know well" |
| **Emotional** | Feelings, emotional energy | "I can't be your only source of support. I need you to also talk to a professional" |
| **Time** | Your schedule, rest | "I'm not available after 7pm for work calls" |
| **Digital** | Online presence, availability | "I don't check messages after dinner" |
| **Material** | Money, possessions | "I'm not able to lend money right now" |
| **Intellectual** | Thoughts, opinions | "I see it differently, and that's okay" |
| **Sexual** | Consent, intimacy | "I'm not comfortable with that, and I need you to respect it" |

## Part 3: How to Set Boundaries (Scripts)

### The Formula
**"I [feel/need/prefer] ___. I would like ___."**

Keep it:
- **Clear** (not hints — direct statements)
- **Kind** (firm but respectful)
- **Brief** (no over-explaining or justifying)

### Ready-Made Scripts

#### Family
| Situation | Script |
|-----------|--------|
| Unwanted advice | "I appreciate your concern. I've thought about it and I'm going with my decision." |
| Invasive questions | "I'm not comfortable discussing that. Let's talk about something else." |
| Guilt trips | "I understand you're disappointed. I still need to do what's right for me." |
| Comparisons | "I'd prefer if we didn't compare. I'm on my own path." |

#### Work
| Situation | Script |
|-----------|--------|
| Extra tasks | "I'm at capacity right now. I can take this on if we reprioritise something else." |
| After-hours contact | "I'll address this first thing tomorrow morning." |
| Unreasonable deadline | "To do this well, I'll need until [date]. Would that work?" |
| Taking credit | "I'd appreciate being acknowledged for my contribution to this project." |

#### Friends
| Situation | Script |
|-----------|--------|
| Emotional dumping | "I care about you, but I'm not in a good headspace to support you right now." |
| Last-minute plans | "I appreciate the invite. I need more notice to plan my time." |
| Lending money | "I'm not in a position to lend money. I hope you understand." |
| Toxic behaviour | "The way you spoke to me was hurtful. I need an apology before we move forward." |

## Part 4: Dealing with Pushback

People who benefit from your lack of boundaries will resist when you set them.

### Common Pushback & Responses

| Their Response | Your Response |
|---------------|--------------|
| "You've changed" | "I'm growing. I hope you can support that." |
| Guilt-tripping | "I understand this is different. My boundary still stands." |
| Anger | "I can see you're upset. I still need this boundary." |
| Silent treatment | Allow space. Don't abandon your boundary to fix their reaction. |
| "But we're family" | "Being family means respecting each other's needs." |

### Key Reminders
- You are not responsible for other people's reactions to your boundaries
- A boundary that makes someone angry was probably very necessary
- Discomfort is temporary. Resentment from no boundaries is permanent
- You don't need to explain, justify, or defend your boundary
- "No" is a complete sentence

## The Indian Context

Boundary-setting in Indian culture can feel especially hard because of:
- Collectivist values (family needs before individual needs)
- Respect for elders (saying no feels disrespectful)
- Community expectations (what will people think?)
- Joint family dynamics

**Reframe:** Setting boundaries is not rejecting your culture. It is choosing to participate in relationships in a way that is sustainable and loving — for everyone, including yourself.
