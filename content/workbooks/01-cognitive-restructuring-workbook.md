## What Is Cognitive Restructuring?

Cognitive restructuring is a core technique from Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT). It helps you:
1. **Identify** automatic negative thoughts
2. **Examine** the evidence for and against them
3. **Replace** them with more balanced, realistic thoughts

This is NOT about "positive thinking." It's about **accurate thinking.**

---

## Week 1: Catching Your Thoughts

### Understanding Automatic Thoughts

Automatic thoughts are the quick, reflexive thoughts that pop into your head. They feel like facts, but they're often distorted.

**Example:**
- Situation: Boss sends a short email
- Automatic thought: "She's angry with me. I'm going to get fired."
- Emotion: Anxiety (8/10)
- Reality: She was in a rush between meetings

### Common Thinking Traps (Cognitive Distortions)

| Distortion | What It Sounds Like | Example |
|-----------|---------------------|---------|
| All-or-Nothing | "If it's not perfect, it's a failure" | "I made one mistake, the whole project is ruined" |
| Catastrophising | "The worst will happen" | "This headache is probably a brain tumour" |
| Mind Reading | "I know what they're thinking" | "Everyone at the party thought I was boring" |
| Fortune Telling | "I know this will go wrong" | "There's no point applying, I won't get it" |
| Should Statements | "I should/must/ought to" | "I should be further ahead at my age" |
| Emotional Reasoning | "I feel it, so it must be true" | "I feel stupid, so I must be stupid" |
| Magnifying/Minimising | Blowing up negatives, shrinking positives | "The one criticism matters more than the 10 compliments" |
| Personalisation | "Everything is my fault" | "They cancelled plans because of me" |
| Labelling | Defining yourself by one event | "I failed the test. I'm a failure." |
| Mental Filter | Focusing only on negatives | "The presentation went well but I forgot one point — it was terrible" |

## Week 2: Examining the Evidence

### The Thought Court

Imagine your automatic thought is on trial. You are both the prosecutor AND the defence attorney.

### Evidence Examination Worksheet

**Automatic thought:** _______________________________________________

**How much do I believe this thought? (0–100%):** ____

| Evidence FOR this thought | Evidence AGAINST this thought |
|--------------------------|------------------------------|
| | |
| | |
| | |
| | |

**Questions to find counter-evidence:**
1. What would I tell a friend who had this thought?
2. Have there been times when this thought wasn't true?
3. Am I confusing a thought with a fact?
4. Am I focusing on one detail and ignoring the bigger picture?
5. What's the MOST LIKELY outcome (not worst case)?
6. Will this matter in 5 years? 5 months? 5 weeks?

**Balanced thought:** _______________________________________________

**How much do I believe the original thought now? (0–100%):** ____

**Emotion now (0–10):** ____

### Week 2 Practice

Complete the Evidence Examination Worksheet for **5 different thoughts** this week. Choose thoughts that scored 6+ on the emotion scale.

## Week 3: Building Alternative Thoughts

### The Balanced Thought Formula

A balanced thought is NOT the opposite of your negative thought. It's the **nuanced middle ground**.

**Formula:** "While [acknowledge the concern], it's also true that [evidence-based balance]."

### Examples:

| Automatic Thought | Balanced Thought |
|-------------------|------------------|
| "I'm going to fail this exam" | "While I'm anxious about the exam, I've studied hard and passed exams before" |
| "Nobody likes me" | "While I felt left out today, I have friends who care about me and reach out regularly" |
| "I'm terrible at my job" | "While I made a mistake today, my overall performance reviews have been positive" |
| "My relationship is falling apart" | "While we're going through a rough patch, we've overcome difficulties before and we're both trying" |

## Week 4: Making It Stick

### Behavioural Experiments

Now test your balanced thoughts in real life:

**The Experiment Template:**

1. **Prediction:** What does my anxious mind predict will happen?
   _____________________________________________

2. **Experiment:** What will I do to test this?
   _____________________________________________

3. **Outcome:** What actually happened?
   _____________________________________________

4. **Learning:** What does this teach me about my thinking pattern?
   _____________________________________________

### Example:
1. **Prediction:** "If I share my idea in the meeting, people will think it's stupid."
2. **Experiment:** Share one idea in tomorrow's meeting.
3. **Outcome:** Two people agreed, one had questions, nobody laughed.
4. **Learning:** My mind predicted rejection, but the reality was neutral-to-positive.

### Creating a Coping Card

Write your top 3 balanced thoughts on a card you carry with you:

```
┌──────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  My Coping Card                          │
│                                          │
│  1. Anxiety is uncomfortable but not     │
│     dangerous. It always passes.         │
│                                          │
│  2. I don't need to be perfect to be     │
│     valued. Good enough IS enough.       │
│                                          │
│  3. One bad day doesn't define my life.  │
│     I've survived 100% of my worst days. │
│                                          │
└──────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Week 4 Practice
- Complete 2 behavioural experiments
- Create your coping card
- Review your 4 weeks of thought logs — notice patterns and progress

## After 4 Weeks

If you've completed this workbook:
- You now have a **skill** for life, not just a quick fix
- Keep a thought record in your phone for ongoing practice
- Notice which thinking traps are YOUR patterns — you'll get faster at catching them
- Consider working with a therapist to go deeper into recurring patterns
