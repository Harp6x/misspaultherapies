## Understanding Self-Esteem

Self-esteem is your overall sense of personal worth. It's not about being arrogant — it's about having a realistic, kind relationship with yourself.

**Low self-esteem is NOT a character flaw.** It develops through experiences:
- Critical parenting or caregivers
- Bullying or social rejection
- Academic pressure and comparison
- Cultural messages about what makes someone "worthy"
- Traumatic experiences
- Constant comparison on social media

## Week 2: Challenging the Critic

### Rewriting the Script

For each inner critic statement, create a compassionate response:

| Inner Critic Says | Compassionate Response |
|-------------------|----------------------|
| "You're not good enough" | "I am enough as I am, and I'm always growing" |
| "Everyone is better than you" | "Comparison steals my joy. My journey is unique" |
| "You don't deserve happiness" | "I deserve the same kindness I give to others" |
| "You always mess things up" | "I'm human. Mistakes are how I learn" |

### The Best Friend Test

When your inner critic speaks, ask: **"Would I say this to my best friend?"**

If the answer is no, you don't deserve to hear it either.

### Relapse Prevention

Self-esteem will dip sometimes. When it does:
1. Recognise it: "My inner critic is loud today"
2. Don't fight it: "That's okay. I know how to handle this"
3. Return to basics: Evidence collection, compassion practice
4. Reach out: Talk to someone who sees your worth
