## What Is Emotional Regulation?

Emotional regulation is the ability to:
- **Recognise** what you're feeling
- **Understand** why you're feeling it
- **Respond** in a way that's helpful (rather than reactive)

It does NOT mean suppressing emotions. It means **relating to them skillfully.**

## Week 1: Emotional Awareness

### The Emotion Wheel

Most people can identify "happy," "sad," "angry," and "anxious." But emotions are more nuanced:

**Anger might actually be:** frustration, resentment, betrayal, jealousy, helplessness, boundary violation

**Sadness might actually be:** grief, loneliness, disappointment, rejection, nostalgia, exhaustion

**Anxiety might actually be:** uncertainty, overwhelm, anticipation, guilt, shame, vulnerability

**Happiness might actually be:** relief, gratitude, pride, excitement, contentment, connection

### Emotions in the Body

Map where you feel emotions physically:

- **Anxiety:** Chest tightness, stomach knots, shallow breathing, sweaty palms
- **Anger:** Jaw clenching, fists, heat in face, racing heart
- **Sadness:** Heavy chest, throat tightness, fatigue, heaviness in limbs
- **Shame:** Face flushing, wanting to shrink, looking down, stomach dropping
- **Joy:** Lightness, warmth, energy, smiling without trying

Emotions don't appear from nowhere. There's always a chain:

```
TRIGGER → INTERPRETATION → EMOTION → URGE → BEHAVIOUR
```

## Week 3: Regulation Strategies

### The Window of Tolerance

You have a "window" where you can feel emotions without being overwhelmed:

```
HYPERAROUSAL (above the window)
— Racing thoughts, panic, rage, can't sit still
═══════════════════════════════════════
THE WINDOW OF TOLERANCE
— You can feel emotions AND think clearly
— You can respond rather than react
═══════════════════════════════════════
HYPOAROUSAL (below the window)
— Numbness, shutdown, dissociation, flatness
```

**Goal:** Widen your window over time so more emotions fit inside it.

### Strategies for Hyperarousal (Too Much Activation)

When you're above the window — racing, panicking, raging:

1. **Cold water on face/wrists** — activates the dive reflex
2. **Slow exhale breathing** — exhale LONGER than inhale (in 4, out 6)
3. **Grounding: 5-4-3-2-1** — engage your senses
4. **Intense physical movement** — 30 seconds of jumping jacks or wall push-ups
5. **Ice cube in hand** — strong sensation brings you into the present

### Strategies for Hypoarousal (Shutdown/Numbness)

When you're below the window — numb, detached, frozen:

1. **Movement** — stand up, walk, stretch, shake your hands
2. **Temperature change** — warm drink, warm shower
3. **Engage senses** — strong flavour (ginger, lemon), strong smell (essential oil)
4. **Orienting** — look around the room, name objects, touch textures
5. **Social connection** — call someone, even briefly

### The TIPP Technique (DBT)

For emotional crises:

| Letter | Technique | How |
|--------|-----------|-----|
| **T** | Temperature | Cold water on face for 30 seconds |
| **I** | Intense exercise | Sprint, jumping jacks, stairs — 10 minutes |
| **P** | Paced breathing | In for 4, out for 6, for 2 minutes |
| **P** | Progressive relaxation | Tense and release each muscle group |

### The STOP Skill

Before reacting to ANY strong emotion:

- **S** — Stop. Freeze. Don't act on the urge.
- **T** — Take a step back. Take a breath.
- **O** — Observe. What am I feeling? What am I thinking? What's the urge?
- **P** — Proceed mindfully. Choose a response (not a reaction).

### Opposite Action

When your emotion drives you toward a harmful behaviour, do the **opposite**:

| Emotion | Urge | Opposite Action |
|---------|------|-----------------|
| Sadness | Withdraw, isolate | Reach out to someone, go outside |
| Anger | Attack, yell | Speak softly, walk away gently |
| Shame | Hide, avoid | Share with a trusted person |
| Anxiety | Avoid the situation | Approach it gradually |
| Guilt | Punish yourself | Practice self-compassion |
