## Understanding the Adolescent Brain
The prefrontal cortex (logic, planning, impulse control) is still developing until age 25. The amygdala (emotions, reactivity) is in overdrive. Your teen isn't being difficult on purpose — their brain is under construction.

## What Your Teen Needs From You

### Autonomy (Gradually Increasing)
- Give age-appropriate choices and independence
- Allow them to make (safe) mistakes and learn
- Resist the urge to control every decision

### Connection (Without Invasion)
- Be available without being intrusive
- Show interest in their world (music, friends, interests)
- Regular 1:1 time without agenda
- Listen more than you lecture

### Boundaries (Firm but Warm)
- Clear expectations with explained reasoning
- Natural consequences rather than punishment
- Consistency without rigidity
- "I trust you AND I need to keep you safe"

## Common Mistakes Parents Make
- Overreacting to normal developmental behaviour
- Comparing with other teens or siblings
- Invading privacy without cause (diary, phone, messages)
- Lecturing instead of listening
- Making their academics YOUR identity
- Dismissing their emotions ("You don't know real problems")
- Using guilt as a control tool

## When to Worry (Red Flags)
- Sudden personality change
- Academic decline without explanation
- Social withdrawal
- Self-harm or talk of death
- Substance use
- Extreme anger or aggression
- Rapid weight loss/gain
- Sleeping all day or not at all

## How to Talk About Difficult Topics
- Choose the right moment (car rides, walks — no eye contact needed)
- Start with curiosity: "I've been wondering..." not "We need to talk"
- Listen fully before responding
- Validate before advising
- Share your own struggles (age-appropriately)
- Don't shame — they'll stop talking to you
