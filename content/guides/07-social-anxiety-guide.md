## What Is Social Anxiety?

Social anxiety disorder is more than shyness. It's an intense, persistent fear of being watched, judged, or humiliated in social situations. It affects approximately 7–12% of the population and often starts in adolescence.

## The Social Anxiety Thought Cycle

```
Anticipation → Catastrophic predictions → Avoidance OR endurance with distress
→ Post-event rumination → Confirmation bias ("I knew it would go badly")
→ More avoidance next time
```

## Common Triggers in Indian Context

- Speaking in front of groups (office meetings, family gatherings)
- Small talk at weddings, parties, and social functions
- Interactions with authority figures (bosses, teachers, in-laws)
- Phone calls (especially to strangers or officials)
- Being the centre of attention (birthday celebrations, achievements)
- Eating in public (office lunches, restaurants)
- Marriage-related social scrutiny ("rishta" meetings)

## Core Techniques

### 1. Cognitive Restructuring
**Identify:** What am I predicting? ("They'll think I'm boring")
**Evaluate:** What's the evidence? (Have people actually said this?)
**Reframe:** What's more realistic? ("Some will find me interesting, some won't — that's normal")
**Test:** Go to the event and check — did the prediction come true?

### 2. The Spotlight Effect
Research shows we massively overestimate how much others notice us. People are mostly focused on themselves. That blushing you noticed? Others probably didn't.

### 3. Attention Training
Social anxiety directs attention INWARD (monitoring yourself). Practice directing it OUTWARD:
- What colour are the other person's eyes?
- What are they saying? (Really listen)
- What's happening in the room?

### 4. Exposure Hierarchy

Build your ladder from least to most anxiety-provoking:

| Step | Situation | Predicted Anxiety | Actual Anxiety | What Happened |
|------|-----------|------------------|---------------|---------------|
| 1 | Make eye contact with a shopkeeper | | | |
| 2 | Ask a stranger for directions | | | |
| 3 | Make a phone call to a business | | | |
| 4 | Speak up once in a meeting | | | |
| 5 | Attend a social event for 30 minutes | | | |
| 6 | Start a conversation with someone new | | | |
| 7 | Give a short presentation | | | |

### 5. Drop Safety Behaviours

Common safety behaviours that maintain social anxiety:
- Rehearsing what to say → prevents natural conversation
- Avoiding eye contact → makes you seem disinterested
- Staying quiet → confirms belief that you have nothing to say
- Over-preparing → exhausting and unsustainable
- Checking phone → prevents real engagement

Experiment: Try dropping ONE safety behaviour at a time and notice what happens.

### 6. Post-Event Processing

After a social event, your brain will want to replay every moment. Instead:
- Set a 5-minute timer — replay is allowed for 5 minutes only
- Write down what went WELL (even small things)
- Ask: "Did the catastrophe actually happen?"
- Move to another activity

## Daily Practice

- One small social exposure per day (even ordering coffee counts)
- 5 minutes of attention training (outward focus)
- Journal one social success each evening
- Practice self-compassion for anxious moments

## When to Seek Therapy

If social anxiety is causing you to: avoid opportunities, decline invitations regularly, or feel persistent distress in social situations — CBT for social anxiety is highly effective.
