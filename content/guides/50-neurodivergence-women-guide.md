# Neurodivergence in Women — Late Diagnosis & Unmasking
### Ms Paul Therapies | @mspaultherapies

---

## Why Women Are Diagnosed Late

ADHD, autism, and other neurodivergent conditions were studied primarily in boys. Women present differently: less disruptive, more internalising, better at masking. The result: girls are missed, and women are diagnosed in their 20s, 30s, or later.

## ADHD in Women
- Inattentive presentation (not hyperactive)
- Appears as "dreamy," "spacey," or "emotional"
- Compensated through anxiety, perfectionism, and overwork
- Diagnosed as depression/anxiety when ADHD is the root cause
- Symptoms worsen with hormonal changes (periods, pregnancy, menopause)
- Rejection Sensitive Dysphoria (intense emotional pain from perceived rejection)

## Autism in Women
- Strong masking ability (copying social behaviour to fit in)
- Special interests that are "socially acceptable" (psychology, animals, art)
- Intense friendships rather than large social circles
- Sensory sensitivities dismissed as "being fussy"
- Exhaustion from the performance of "normalcy"
- Meltdowns in private, composed in public
- Often misdiagnosed as: anxiety, BPD, depression, eating disorders

## The Unmasking Process
When you discover you're neurodivergent:
1. **Grief** — for the years of struggle without understanding
2. **Relief** — "There's a reason I've always felt different"
3. **Anger** — at the systems that missed you
4. **Identity reconstruction** — who am I without the mask?
5. **Integration** — accepting all parts of yourself

## What Unmasking Looks Like
- Allowing yourself to stim (fidget, rock, tap)
- Saying "I need quiet" without guilt
- Declining events that drain you
- Pursuing interests without shame
- Setting up your environment for YOUR brain
- Asking for accommodations at work

## Getting Assessed
- Seek a professional experienced with adult female neurodivergence
- Self-assessment tools are useful but not diagnostic
- A thorough clinical interview + developmental history is needed
- Late diagnosis is valid — it doesn't change who you are

## Indian Context
- Neurodivergence barely discussed in India
- Girls expected to be quiet and compliant (masking rewarded)
- Academic success used as evidence against diagnosis
- "She's just shy/emotional/sensitive" delays evaluation
- Limited access to neurodivergence-informed professionals
