## What Is a Phobia?

A phobia is an intense, irrational fear of a specific object or situation that is disproportionate to the actual danger. You know the fear is excessive — but you can't control it.

## Common Phobias

| Phobia | Fear Of |
|--------|---------|
| Arachnophobia | Spiders |
| Acrophobia | Heights |
| Claustrophobia | Enclosed spaces |
| Agoraphobia | Open/crowded spaces or leaving home |
| Trypanophobia | Needles/injections |
| Aerophobia | Flying |
| Cynophobia | Dogs |
| Social phobia | Social situations |
| Hemophobia | Blood |
| Emetophobia | Vomiting |
| Thanatophobia | Death |
| Dentophobia | Dentists |

## How Phobias Develop

1. **Direct experience:** A traumatic event involving the feared object
2. **Observational learning:** Watching someone else be afraid
3. **Informational learning:** Being told something is dangerous
4. **Evolutionary preparedness:** Some fears are "pre-programmed" (snakes, heights, darkness)

## The Avoidance Trap

```
Fear → Avoidance → Relief (short-term) → Fear stays or grows
→ Life shrinks → More avoidance → More fear
```

Avoidance MAINTAINS phobias. Exposure TREATS them.

## Treatment: Graded Exposure

The most effective treatment is systematic desensitisation — gradually facing the feared stimulus:

### Example: Dog Phobia

| Step | Exposure | Anxiety (0-10) |
|------|----------|---------------|
| 1 | Look at photos of dogs | 3 |
| 2 | Watch videos of dogs | 4 |
| 3 | Be in same room as a small dog (far away) | 5 |
| 4 | Be near a calm dog on a leash | 6 |
| 5 | Sit next to someone holding a small dog | 7 |
| 6 | Touch a calm dog briefly | 8 |
| 7 | Pet a dog for 1 minute | 8 |
| 8 | Be near a dog without a leash | 9 |

### Rules of Exposure
1. Start at the bottom of your hierarchy
2. Stay in the situation until anxiety drops by at least 50%
3. Don't use safety behaviours (closing eyes, gripping, escaping)
4. Repeat each step until it feels manageable
5. Move to the next step only when ready

## Phobia Treatment Has the Highest Success Rate in Psychology

70-90% of people with specific phobias achieve significant improvement with exposure therapy. Some phobias can be treated in a single extended session.
