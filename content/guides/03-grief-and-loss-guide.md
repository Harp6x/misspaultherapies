## What Is Grief?

Grief is the natural response to losing someone or something that mattered to you. It is not limited to death — you can grieve:
- A relationship ending
- A job or career change
- A miscarriage or fertility struggle
- A friendship that faded
- Moving away from home
- Loss of health or ability
- Loss of a dream or expectation
- A pet passing away

**There is no "correct" way to grieve. Your grief is valid, regardless of what others think.**

## Part 1: The Landscape of Grief

### Common Grief Experiences

**Emotional:**
- Deep sadness, emptiness, or numbness
- Anger (at the person, at yourself, at life, at God)
- Guilt ("I should have done more")
- Relief (especially after prolonged illness — this is normal)
- Anxiety about the future
- Longing and yearning

**Physical:**
- Exhaustion and fatigue
- Difficulty sleeping or sleeping too much
- Changes in appetite
- Physical aches (grief literally hurts)
- Weakened immune system
- Tightness in chest or throat

**Cognitive:**
- Difficulty concentrating
- Forgetfulness
- Disbelief ("This can't be real")
- Searching for meaning
- Replaying memories

**Behavioural:**
- Withdrawing from others
- Crying unexpectedly
- Restlessness
- Avoiding reminders — or seeking them out
- Changes in routine

**All of these are normal.**

## Part 2: Myths About Grief

| Myth | Truth |
|------|-------|
| "You should be over it by now" | Grief has no timeline. It changes shape but may never fully "end." |
| "Stay strong for others" | Allowing yourself to feel is not weakness — it is processing. |
| "There are 5 stages and they happen in order" | Grief is not linear. You may cycle through feelings many times. |
| "Keeping busy will help" | Distraction helps short-term but avoidance delays healing. |
| "If you're laughing, you're not grieving properly" | Joy and grief can coexist. Laughing does not mean you don't care. |
| "Grief only happens after death" | Any significant loss can trigger grief. |

## Part 3: Navigating Grief Day by Day

### The "Good Enough" Approach

On hard days, your only goal is to get through the day. That is enough.

**Minimum daily self-care during grief:**
- [ ] Drink water
- [ ] Eat something (even if small)
- [ ] Step outside for 5 minutes
- [ ] Talk to one person (even a text counts)
- [ ] Allow yourself to feel whatever comes up

### When Grief Waves Hit

Grief comes in waves — sometimes triggered, sometimes not.

**When a wave hits:**
1. **Pause.** Don't fight it.
2. **Name it.** "I'm feeling grief right now."
3. **Ground yourself.** Feel your feet on the floor. Take slow breaths.
4. **Let it move through.** Waves rise, peak, and fall. This one will too.
5. **Be gentle after.** Rest if you need to.

### Creating Rituals

Rituals help give grief a place to go:
- Light a candle on significant dates
- Write letters to the person you've lost
- Create a memory box or photo album
- Visit a meaningful place
- Cook their favourite meal
- Plant something in their memory

## Part 4: Supporting Yourself Through Grief

### Journaling Prompts for Grief

Choose one when you feel ready:
1. "Today I miss you because..."
2. "Something I wish I had said is..."
3. "A favourite memory I want to hold onto is..."
4. "Right now I feel... and that's okay because..."
5. "Something that helped me today was..."
6. "If I could tell you one thing, it would be..."
7. "I'm learning that grief feels like..."

### Things You Can Say to Yourself

- "It's okay to not be okay right now."
- "My grief is a measure of my love."
- "I don't have to have it all figured out today."
- "I can feel sad and still move forward."
- "Healing doesn't mean forgetting."

### Things Others Might Say (and How to Respond)

| What they say | What you can say |
|---------------|-----------------|
| "They're in a better place" | "I appreciate you care. Right now I just miss them." |
| "At least..." | "I know you mean well. This is just really hard." |
| "Stay strong" | "I'm trying. Some days that means letting myself cry." |
| "Let me know if you need anything" | "Actually, could you [specific thing]? That would help." |

## Part 5: When Grief Needs Professional Support

Consider reaching out if:
- [ ] You feel stuck in grief for many months with no movement
- [ ] You're unable to function at work or in daily life
- [ ] You're having thoughts of self-harm or joining the person who died
- [ ] You're using substances to numb the pain
- [ ] You feel intense guilt that won't ease
- [ ] Your relationships are significantly suffering
- [ ] You're experiencing complicated grief (persistent longing that dominates your life)

**Grief therapy is not about "getting over" your loss. It's about learning to carry it in a way that allows you to also live.**

## Part 6: Cultural Sensitivity Note

In Indian culture, grief often intersects with:
- Family expectations about mourning periods
- Religious rituals and ceremonies
- Pressure to "move on" after appropriate time
- Gendered expectations (men shouldn't cry, women should be resilient for the family)
- Joint family dynamics affecting private grieving

**Your grief is yours.** You can honour cultural traditions while also honouring your own emotional needs. These are not in conflict.
