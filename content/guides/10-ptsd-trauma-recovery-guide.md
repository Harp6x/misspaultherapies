## Understanding Trauma

Trauma isn't defined by the event — it's defined by your nervous system's response to the event. Two people can experience the same event and respond differently. Both responses are valid.

### Types of Trauma
- **Acute trauma:** Single event (accident, assault, natural disaster)
- **Complex trauma:** Repeated, prolonged exposure (childhood abuse, domestic violence)
- **Developmental trauma:** Early childhood neglect or disruption of attachment
- **Vicarious trauma:** Absorbing others' trauma (caregivers, therapists, witnesses)
- **Intergenerational trauma:** Patterns passed down through families

## How Trauma Lives in the Body

Trauma isn't just a memory — it's stored in your nervous system:
- **Hyperarousal:** Always on alert, jumpy, can't relax
- **Hypoarousal:** Numb, disconnected, "checked out"
- **Window of tolerance:** The zone between these extremes where you can function

Recovery involves expanding your window of tolerance so you can stay present more often.

## The Three Phases of Trauma Recovery

### Phase 1: Safety & Stabilisation
- Establish physical safety
- Build a therapeutic relationship
- Learn grounding and regulation skills
- Address basic needs (sleep, nutrition, routine)
- Develop a safety plan for crisis moments

### Phase 2: Processing
- Gradually approach traumatic memories
- Make meaning of what happened
- Process emotions (grief, anger, shame)
- Challenge trauma-related beliefs ("It was my fault," "I'm damaged")
- Techniques: EMDR, trauma-focused CBT, somatic experiencing

### Phase 3: Reconnection
- Rebuild relationships and trust
- Rediscover identity beyond trauma
- Engage in meaningful activities
- Post-traumatic growth — many survivors find deeper meaning, compassion, and strength

## Grounding Techniques for Flashbacks

### 5-4-3-2-1 Technique
- 5 things you can SEE
- 4 things you can TOUCH
- 3 things you can HEAR
- 2 things you can SMELL
- 1 thing you can TASTE

### Orienting
- Name today's date, your age, where you are
- Look around the room and name objects
- "That was then. This is now. I am safe."

### Body-Based
- Press feet firmly into the ground
- Hold ice cubes or splash cold water on your face
- Cross your arms and tap alternately on each shoulder (butterfly hug)

## Trauma in the Indian Context

- Childhood physical discipline normalised but can be traumatic
- Sexual abuse often hidden due to family "honour" (izzat)
- Domestic violence tolerated within marriage
- Natural disasters and communal violence create community-wide trauma
- "Move on" and "be strong" culture prevents processing
- Spiritual bypassing ("It was your karma")

## What You Need to Know

1. **It wasn't your fault** — regardless of what anyone told you
2. **Your reactions make sense** — they were survival strategies
3. **Healing is possible** — but it's not linear
4. **You don't have to do this alone** — trauma-informed therapy exists
5. **There is no timeline** — your pace is the right pace
