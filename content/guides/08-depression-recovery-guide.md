## Understanding Depression

Depression is not a character flaw, laziness, or weakness. It's a medical condition involving changes in brain chemistry, thought patterns, and behaviour. It affects how you feel, think, and function.

## The Depression Trap

Depression creates a vicious cycle:
```
Low mood → Withdrawal from activities → Less pleasure/achievement
→ More negative thinking → Lower mood → More withdrawal
```

Recovery works by breaking this cycle at multiple points.

## Phase 1: Surviving (When Everything Feels Impossible)

### The Absolute Basics Checklist
When depression is severe, "self-care" means survival:
- ☐ Get out of bed (even if you get back in later)
- ☐ Drink water
- ☐ Eat something (anything — perfection is not the goal)
- ☐ Shower or wash your face
- ☐ Open a curtain for natural light
- ☐ Send one text to one person
- ☐ Take medication if prescribed

**That's enough. Every single one of these is an achievement when you're depressed.**

## Phase 2: Stabilising (Building Small Routines)

### Behavioural Activation — The Core of Depression Recovery

Depression tells you to wait until you "feel like" doing things. The research says the opposite: **action comes before motivation, not after.**

#### Activity Scheduling
Plan 1–2 small activities per day that provide:
- **Pleasure** (P): Things that feel good (watching a show, petting your dog)
- **Achievement** (A): Things that give a sense of accomplishment (replying to an email, cooking a meal)

| Time | Activity | P or A? | Mood Before (1-10) | Mood After (1-10) |
|------|----------|---------|--------------------|--------------------|
| Morning | | | | |
| Afternoon | | | | |
| Evening | | | | |

### Sleep Hygiene for Depression
- Wake at the same time daily (even weekends)
- No naps longer than 20 minutes
- No screens 30 minutes before bed
- Bed is for sleep only (not scrolling, working, or ruminating)

### Movement
Exercise is as effective as antidepressants for mild-moderate depression. Start with:
- 10-minute walk (that's it — no gym required)
- Build to 20–30 minutes, 3–4 times per week

## Phase 3: Rebuilding (Changing Thought Patterns)

### Cognitive Restructuring
Depression distorts thinking. Common patterns:

| Distortion | Example | Challenge |
|-----------|---------|-----------|
| All-or-nothing | "If I can't do it perfectly, why bother?" | "Doing it imperfectly is better than not doing it" |
| Catastrophising | "Everything is falling apart" | "What specifically is going wrong? What's still okay?" |
| Mind-reading | "They think I'm pathetic" | "I don't know what they think. What's the evidence?" |
| Discounting positives | "That doesn't count" | "Why doesn't it count? Would I say this to a friend?" |
| Should statements | "I should be over this by now" | "According to whom? Recovery takes time" |

### Gratitude Practice (Simple Version)
Each night, write 3 things:
1. Something that went okay today
2. Something you did (even small)
3. Something you're grateful for

## Phase 4: Thriving (Long-Term Maintenance)

### Relapse Prevention
- Know your warning signs (the first things that slip)
- Maintain medication if prescribed (don't stop without doctor guidance)
- Keep therapy appointments even when feeling better
- Maintain exercise, sleep, and social routines
- Have a crisis plan ready

### Meaning & Purpose
Depression recovery isn't just about removing symptoms — it's about building a life worth living:
- What are your values?
- What brings you meaning?
- What relationships matter most?
- What legacy do you want to create?

## For Loved Ones

- Don't say: "Just be positive" / "Others have it worse" / "Snap out of it"
- Do say: "I'm here" / "You don't have to explain" / "What do you need?"
- Be patient — recovery is not linear
- Take care of YOUR mental health too
