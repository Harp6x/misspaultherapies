# Postpartum Mental Health — A Complete Guide for New Parents
### Ms Paul Therapies | @mspaultherapies

## Beyond Baby Blues

Baby blues (mood swings, crying, anxiety) affect 80% of new mothers and resolve within 2 weeks. When symptoms persist or worsen, it may be a postpartum mood disorder.

## Postpartum Conditions

### Postpartum Depression (PPD)
- Persistent sadness, guilt, hopelessness
- Difficulty bonding with the baby
- Loss of interest, appetite, or energy
- Affects 15-20% of new mothers
- Can affect fathers too (10%)

### Postpartum Anxiety (PPA)
- Excessive worry about baby's health/safety
- Racing thoughts, restlessness
- Difficulty sleeping even when baby sleeps
- Intrusive "what if" scenarios

### Postpartum OCD
- Intrusive, horrifying thoughts about harming the baby (ego-dystonic — you DON'T want to)
- Compulsive checking, avoiding being alone with baby
- Extreme guilt and shame about the thoughts
- **These thoughts do NOT mean you will act on them**

### Postpartum Psychosis (Rare but Urgent)
- Confusion, hallucinations, delusions
- Rapid mood swings, paranoia
- **Medical emergency — call 112 or go to ER immediately**

## Why It Happens

- Hormonal crash after delivery (estrogen drops 1000x)
- Sleep deprivation
- Identity shift
- Relationship changes
- Unrealistic expectations of motherhood
- Lack of support
- History of depression/anxiety
- Traumatic birth experience

## Indian Context

- "A new mother should be happy" — cultural pressure
- Joint family can help but also creates pressure and criticism
- Confinement practices (40 days) — beneficial rest OR isolating imprisonment
- Mother-in-law dynamics during postpartum
- Pressure for a male child creating additional distress
- Breastfeeding pressure and guilt

## What Helps

### For the Mother
1. **Accept help** — you cannot and should not do this alone
2. **Sleep** — prioritise it. Accept imperfection elsewhere
3. **Talk** — to your partner, friend, therapist, helpline
4. **Move** — even a 10-minute walk makes a difference
5. **Lower the bar** — "good enough" parenting is perfect parenting
6. **Take medication if needed** — safe options exist for breastfeeding

### For Partners
1. Take over baby duties to let her sleep
2. Ask "How are YOU?" (not just about the baby)
3. Don't minimise ("Other women manage")
4. Protect her from unwanted visitors/advice
5. Get educated about PPD
6. Get your own support — you're transitioning too

## When to Seek Help Immediately

- Thoughts of harming yourself or the baby
- Inability to care for the baby at all
- Feeling disconnected from reality
- Hearing/seeing things that aren't there
- Severe anxiety that doesn't let you function
