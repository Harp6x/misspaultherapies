## What Is Intergenerational Trauma?

Trauma that's passed down through families — not genetically, but through parenting patterns, family dynamics, attachment styles, and epigenetic changes. Your parents' unprocessed pain becomes your emotional inheritance.

## How Trauma Transfers

1. **Parenting patterns:** Traumatised parents may be emotionally unavailable, hypervigilant, controlling, or volatile
2. **Attachment disruption:** Insecure attachment in parent → insecure attachment in child
3. **Family narratives:** "We don't talk about that," "Be strong," silence around painful events
4. **Emotional modelling:** Children learn to suppress, explode, or disconnect from emotions
5. **Epigenetics:** Stress responses can alter gene expression across generations

## Common Sources in Indian Families
- **Partition trauma** (1947) — displacement, violence, loss
- **Caste-based discrimination** — generations of oppression and internalised shame
- **Poverty and financial insecurity** — scarcity mindset passed down
- **Gender-based violence** — normalised domestic violence across generations
- **Colonial legacy** — internalised inferiority, cultural shame
- **Religious/communal violence** — unspoken community grief
- **Forced migration** — loss of homeland, language, community

## Signs You're Carrying Intergenerational Trauma
- Anxiety or hypervigilance without a clear personal cause
- Patterns of relationship dysfunction that mirror your parents'
- Emotional responses that feel "bigger than the situation"
- Difficulty trusting despite no personal betrayal
- Scarcity mindset despite financial stability
- Repeating cycles you swore you'd break
- Carrying shame about your family, background, or identity
- Strong avoidance of certain topics, places, or memories

## Breaking the Cycle

### 1. Awareness
"This pattern didn't start with me. I'm carrying something that predates my experience."

### 2. Understanding Your Parents
Not excusing — understanding. What did THEY experience? What tools did they have? What was denied to them?

### 3. Processing Your Own Pain
Therapy to separate YOUR story from your family's story. Feel what you've been carrying. Grieve what you missed.

### 4. Reparenting Yourself
Give yourself what your parents couldn't:
- Emotional validation
- Consistent safety
- Permission to feel
- Unconditional acceptance

### 5. Choosing Differently
With your own children, in your relationships, in how you treat yourself — each conscious choice breaks a link in the chain.

## The Most Powerful Act of Healing

"I will not pass this on." Breaking the cycle doesn't just heal you — it heals forward.
