## Why This Guide Exists

Indian work culture has unique pressures:
- Long hours and "always-on" expectations
- Family obligations alongside career demands
- Cultural stigma around mental health
- Competitive environments from school onwards
- Pressure to "adjust" rather than advocate for yourself

Self-care is not selfish. It is the foundation that allows you to show up for everything else.

## Part 1: The Burnout Check

Rate each statement from 1 (rarely) to 5 (almost always):

| # | Statement | Score |
|---|-----------|-------|
| 1 | I feel exhausted even after sleeping | ___ |
| 2 | I dread Monday mornings | ___ |
| 3 | I feel cynical or detached from my work | ___ |
| 4 | I struggle to find meaning in what I do | ___ |
| 5 | I snap at colleagues or family easily | ___ |
| 6 | I work through lunch or skip meals regularly | ___ |
| 7 | I check work messages before bed and first thing in the morning | ___ |
| 8 | I feel guilty when I'm not being productive | ___ |
| 9 | I haven't taken a proper day off in weeks | ___ |
| 10 | I feel like I'm running on empty | ___ |

**Score: ___ / 50**

- **10–20:** You're managing well. Maintain your current practices.
- **21–35:** Warning zone. Start implementing the strategies below.
- **36–50:** High burnout risk. Prioritise rest and consider professional support.

## Part 2: Micro Self-Care (5 Minutes or Less)

You don't need a spa day. These take under 5 minutes:

### At Your Desk
- **Box breathing:** Inhale 4s, hold 4s, exhale 4s, hold 4s. Repeat 4 times.
- **Desk stretch:** Roll your neck, stretch your wrists, stand and stretch your back
- **Gratitude pause:** Write 3 things that went well today
- **Hydration check:** Drink a full glass of water
- **Eye rest:** 20-20-20 rule — every 20 minutes, look at something 20 feet away for 20 seconds

### During Commute
- Listen to music that calms you (not news)
- Practice 5-4-3-2-1 grounding
- Do a mental "body scan" — notice where you hold tension
- Set an intention for the day (morning) or do a gratitude review (evening)

### At Home
- 5-minute chai break with NO phone
- Step onto your balcony and look at the sky
- Pet your dog/cat for 5 minutes (proven to lower cortisol)
- Do 10 slow deep breaths before entering the house

## Part 3: Boundaries at Work

### The Art of Saying No (Professionally)

| Situation | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| Extra work dumped on you | "I'd like to help, but I'm at capacity this week. Can we discuss priorities?" |
| After-hours messages | "I'll look at this first thing tomorrow morning." |
| Weekend calls | "I keep weekends for family time. Let's connect Monday." |
| Unreasonable deadline | "I want to do this well. To deliver quality, I'd need until [date]. Would that work?" |

### Protecting Your Time
- Block "focus time" on your calendar (treat it like a meeting)
- Turn off email notifications during deep work
- Don't eat lunch at your desk — take a real break
- Set a hard stop time at least 3 days a week

### The Indian Context
In Indian workplaces, boundaries can feel disrespectful. Reframe it:
- Boundaries are not about saying "I don't care"
- Boundaries are about saying "I care enough to be sustainable"
- A burned-out employee helps nobody
- You can be respectful AND firm

## Part 4: Managing the Guilt

### Common Guilt Triggers

| Guilt | Reframe |
|-------|---------|
| "I should work harder than everyone else" | "Working smarter and resting is not laziness" |
| "My parents sacrificed so much — I can't complain" | "Gratitude and burnout can coexist" |
| "Everyone else seems fine" | "Most people are struggling silently too" |
| "Taking time off is selfish" | "Rest makes me better at everything I do" |
| "I should be available 24/7" | "My best work comes when I'm recharged" |

### Permission Slips

You have permission to:
- Leave work on time
- Not answer messages instantly
- Say "I need a break"
- Prioritise sleep over late-night deadlines
- Not have it all figured out
- Feel tired even if others have it "worse"
- Ask for help

## Part 5: Weekly Self-Care Menu

Pick at least ONE from each category per week:

### Physical
- [ ] 30-minute walk or exercise
- [ ] Cook a meal from scratch
- [ ] Sleep 7+ hours for 5 nights
- [ ] Stretch for 10 minutes
- [ ] Drink 8 glasses of water daily

### Emotional
- [ ] Journal for 10 minutes
- [ ] Call a friend who makes you laugh
- [ ] Cry if you need to (it's a release, not a weakness)
- [ ] Say no to one thing you don't want to do
- [ ] Express one feeling to someone you trust

### Social
- [ ] Have one meal with family/friends (phone-free)
- [ ] Send a message to someone you've been thinking about
- [ ] Join a hobby class or community
- [ ] Have a real conversation (not just small talk)

### Mental
- [ ] Read something for pleasure (not work)
- [ ] Do a digital detox for 2 hours
- [ ] Learn something new (just for fun)
- [ ] Practice mindfulness for 5 minutes
- [ ] Write down 3 things you're proud of this week

## Part 6: Emergency Self-Care for Bad Days

When everything feels too much:

1. **Get horizontal.** Lie down. You don't need to "push through."
2. **Cold water on face.** Activates the dive reflex, calms your nervous system.
3. **Text one person.** "I'm having a hard day." You don't need to explain more.
4. **Eat something.** Your brain needs fuel. Even a biscuit and chai.
5. **Step outside.** 60 seconds of sunlight changes your brain chemistry.
6. **Lower the bar.** Today's only job is to get through today.
