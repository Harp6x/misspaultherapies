## What Is Panic Disorder?

Panic disorder involves recurrent, unexpected panic attacks — sudden surges of intense fear that peak within minutes. Unlike normal anxiety, panic attacks feel like a medical emergency: racing heart, chest pain, shortness of breath, dizziness, and an overwhelming fear of dying or losing control.

The disorder develops when you begin to fear the attacks themselves, leading to avoidance of situations where attacks have occurred or might occur.

## The Anatomy of a Panic Attack

1. **Trigger** (often internal — a body sensation, thought, or nothing at all)
2. **Catastrophic interpretation** ("I'm having a heart attack," "I'm going crazy")
3. **Fight-or-flight activation** (adrenaline surge)
4. **Physical symptoms** intensify (which seems to confirm the catastrophic thought)
5. **Peak** (usually 5–10 minutes)
6. **Gradual decline** (20–30 minutes total)
7. **Exhaustion and fear** of the next one

## Why Panic Attacks Are Not Dangerous

Despite how terrifying they feel:
- Your heart is designed to beat fast under stress — it won't give out
- You cannot stop breathing during a panic attack (hyperventilation feels like suffocation but isn't)
- You will not faint (blood pressure rises during panic, fainting requires a drop)
- You are not going crazy — you are experiencing a misfiring alarm system
- No one has ever died from a panic attack

## The Panic Cycle and How to Break It

### The Maintaining Cycle
```
Panic attack → Fear of another attack → Hypervigilance to body sensations
→ Avoidance of triggers → Life shrinks → More anxiety → More panic attacks
```

### Breaking the Cycle
1. **Psychoeducation:** Understanding that panic is a false alarm reduces fear
2. **Interoceptive exposure:** Deliberately inducing safe versions of panic sensations (spinning for dizziness, breathing through a straw for breathlessness) to desensitise
3. **Cognitive restructuring:** Challenging catastrophic interpretations
4. **Behavioural experiments:** Testing feared predictions ("If I go to the mall, I'll have a panic attack and everyone will stare")
5. **Dropping safety behaviours:** Gradually reducing things you do to feel "safe" (carrying medication, sitting near exits, having your phone ready)

## Evidence-Based Techniques

### 1. Breathing Retraining
Panic often involves hyperventilation. Practice diaphragmatic breathing daily:
- Place one hand on chest, one on belly
- Breathe so only the belly hand moves
- Inhale 4 counts, exhale 6 counts
- Practice 5 minutes daily (NOT just during panic)

### 2. Cognitive Defusion
Instead of "I'm going to die," try:
- "I notice I'm having the thought that I'm going to die"
- "My brain is sending a false alarm right now"
- "This is anxiety, not reality"

### 3. Progressive Muscle Relaxation
Systematically tense and release muscle groups:
- Feet → calves → thighs → stomach → chest → arms → shoulders → face
- Tense for 5 seconds, release for 10 seconds each
- Practice daily for prevention, not just during panic

### 4. Graded Exposure
List avoided situations from least to most feared. Gradually face them:

| 5 | [Hardest] | | |

## Lifestyle Foundations

- **Reduce caffeine** — it mimics anxiety symptoms
- **Regular exercise** — 30 minutes, 4x per week reduces panic frequency
- **Consistent sleep** — sleep deprivation lowers panic threshold
- **Limit alcohol** — may reduce anxiety short-term but increases it long-term
- **Balanced meals** — blood sugar drops can trigger panic sensations

## When to Seek Professional Help

- Panic attacks happening weekly or more
- Significant avoidance affecting your work, social life, or relationships
- Fear of leaving home (agoraphobia developing)
- Using substances to manage panic
- Depression developing alongside panic

**CBT for panic disorder has a 70–80% success rate.** It's one of the most treatable anxiety conditions.
