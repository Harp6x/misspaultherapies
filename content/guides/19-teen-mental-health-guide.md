## The Adolescent Brain

The teen brain is under massive construction. The prefrontal cortex (decision-making, impulse control) doesn't fully develop until age 25. Meanwhile, the emotional brain (amygdala) is in overdrive. This explains why teens feel everything intensely and sometimes make impulsive decisions.

## Common Mental Health Issues in Indian Teens

### Academic Pressure
- Board exam anxiety (10th and 12th become do-or-die)
- Coaching class burnout (Kota syndrome)
- Parental expectations creating performance anxiety
- Self-worth tied to marks

### Social Media & Technology
- Cyberbullying
- Social comparison and FOMO
- Screen addiction affecting sleep
- Online predators and inappropriate content
- Identity formation through likes and followers

### Identity & Peer Issues
- Peer pressure (substances, relationships, appearance)
- Bullying (physical, verbal, social exclusion)
- Gender identity and sexuality exploration
- Cultural identity conflicts (traditional vs modern)

### Family Dynamics
- Parental conflict or divorce impact
- Authoritarian parenting creating rebellion or suppression
- Over-involvement preventing autonomy development
- Communication breakdown

## Warning Signs (For Parents)

### Mood Changes
- Persistent sadness or irritability beyond normal teen moodiness
- Withdrawal from family and friends
- Loss of interest in activities they used to enjoy
- Excessive anger or emotional outbursts

### Behavioural Changes
- Academic decline
- Sleep pattern changes (too much or too little)
- Appetite changes
- New peer group (especially if secretive)
- Substance use (alcohol, smoking, drugs)
- Self-harm (cuts, burns — check arms, thighs)

### Serious Red Flags
- Talking about death, dying, or being a burden
- Giving away possessions
- Sudden calmness after a period of depression
- Writing notes or social media posts about ending life

**If present → seek immediate professional help. Don't leave them alone.**

## For Parents: How to Talk to Your Teen

### Do
- Listen without interrupting or fixing
- Validate: "That sounds really hard"
- Ask open-ended questions: "Tell me more about that"
- Normalise feelings: "It's okay to feel that way"
- Respect their privacy (while maintaining safety)
- Apologise when you're wrong

### Don't
- Dismiss: "It's just a phase" / "When I was your age..."
- Threaten: "If you don't study, I'll take your phone"
- Compare: "Your cousin scored 95%"
- Interrogate: Turning conversations into investigations
- Violate trust: Reading diaries, monitoring every message

## For Teens: What You Can Do

1. **Talk to someone** — parent, teacher, school counsellor, friend, therapist
2. **It's not forever** — teen years are intense but temporary
3. **Your feelings are valid** — even if adults don't understand
4. **Limit social media** — compare less, live more
5. **Move your body** — exercise genuinely helps mood
6. **Help is not weakness** — it's the strongest thing you can do
