# Attachment Styles — How Your Childhood Shapes Your Relationships
### Ms Paul Therapies | @mspaultherapies

## The Four Attachment Styles

### Secure Attachment (~50% of population)
**Childhood:** Consistent, responsive caregiving
**Adult relationships:** Comfortable with intimacy and independence. Communicates needs directly. Trusts partner. Can self-regulate emotions.

### Anxious Attachment (~20%)
**Childhood:** Inconsistent caregiving (sometimes available, sometimes not)
**Adult relationships:** Fear of abandonment. Needs constant reassurance. Hyper-alert to partner's mood changes. Can become clingy or demanding. Core fear: "I'm too much" or "I'll be left."

### Avoidant Attachment (~25%)
**Childhood:** Emotionally unavailable or dismissive caregiving
**Adult relationships:** Discomfort with closeness. Values independence highly. Shuts down during conflict. Difficulty expressing emotions. Core fear: "I'll be trapped" or "Needing someone makes me weak."

### Disorganised Attachment (~5%)
**Childhood:** Frightening or chaotic caregiving (the source of comfort was also the source of fear)
**Adult relationships:** Push-pull dynamics. Wants closeness but fears it. Can be unpredictable in relationships. Often linked to trauma.

## How Attachment Plays Out

| Situation | Anxious | Avoidant | Secure |
|-----------|---------|----------|--------|
| Partner is late | "They're leaving me" | "I don't care" | "I'll text to check" |
| Conflict arises | Pursue, need to resolve NOW | Withdraw, need space | Discuss calmly |
| Partner needs space | Panic, feel rejected | Relief | Respect + stay connected |
| Expressing needs | Over-express, then feel guilty | Can't identify needs | Clear and direct |

## The Anxious-Avoidant Trap

The most common (and most painful) pairing. The anxious partner pursues → the avoidant partner withdraws → the anxious pursues harder → the avoidant shuts down further. Both are triggered. Neither feels safe.

## Moving Toward Secure

### For Anxious Attachment
- Learn to self-soothe before seeking reassurance
- Develop interests outside the relationship
- Notice the difference between past fear and present reality
- Communicate needs without urgency

### For Avoidant Attachment
- Practice identifying and naming emotions
- Share small vulnerabilities (build tolerance gradually)
- Notice when you withdraw and choose to stay
- Recognise that needing people isn't weakness

### For Everyone
- Attachment style is not destiny — it can change with awareness and effort
- Therapy accelerates this process significantly
- Choose secure partners when possible (they help regulate you)
- Develop "earned security" through self-awareness and healing

## Indian Context

- Emotional availability often limited in Indian parenting styles
- "Don't cry," "Be strong" messaging creates avoidant patterns
- Enmeshed families can create anxious attachment
- Arranged marriages may pair incompatible attachment styles
- Discussing emotions in relationships still seen as "weakness"
