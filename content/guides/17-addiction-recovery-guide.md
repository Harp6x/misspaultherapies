## What Is Addiction?

Addiction is a chronic brain condition — not a moral failing. It involves compulsive substance use or behaviour despite harmful consequences. The brain's reward system gets hijacked, making the substance/behaviour feel essential for survival.

## Types of Addiction

### Substance Addictions
- Alcohol, tobacco, cannabis, prescription drugs (benzodiazepines, opioids), recreational drugs

### Behavioural Addictions
- Gambling, gaming, social media, pornography, shopping, food

## The Cycle of Addiction

```
Trigger (stress, boredom, pain, social pressure)
→ Craving (intense urge)
→ Use (temporary relief/pleasure)
→ Guilt/shame/consequences
→ More triggers → More use
```

## Signs You May Have a Problem

- Using more than intended or for longer than planned
- Failed attempts to cut down or stop
- Significant time spent obtaining, using, or recovering
- Cravings that dominate your thinking
- Neglecting responsibilities (work, family, health)
- Continued use despite relationship problems
- Tolerance (needing more for the same effect)
- Withdrawal symptoms when you stop
- Giving up activities you used to enjoy

## Addiction in India

- Alcohol is the most common substance of abuse
- Rising prescription drug misuse (benzodiazepines, cough syrups)
- Cannabis normalised in some cultural/religious contexts
- Social drinking pressure in professional circles
- Stigma prevents help-seeking, especially for women
- Family often enables or hides the problem

## Recovery Framework

### Stage 1: Acknowledgment
- "I have a problem" — the hardest and most important step
- Not about willpower — about recognising a pattern

### Stage 2: Detox & Stabilisation
- Medical supervision for alcohol/benzodiazepine withdrawal (can be dangerous)
- Manage physical symptoms
- Establish safety

### Stage 3: Therapy & Skill-Building
- **CBT:** Identify triggers, challenge addictive thinking, build coping skills
- **Motivational Interviewing:** Explore ambivalence, build internal motivation
- **12-Step Programs:** AA, NA — community support
- **Family therapy:** Address enabling patterns, rebuild trust

### Stage 4: Relapse Prevention
- Identify high-risk situations
- Build a sober support network
- Develop healthy coping (exercise, hobbies, connection)
- Create a relapse prevention plan
- Understand: relapse is common but not inevitable

## For Families

- Stop enabling (covering up, making excuses, providing money)
- Set boundaries with love
- Attend Al-Anon or family support groups
- Get your own therapy
- You cannot force recovery — but you can stop participating in the cycle
