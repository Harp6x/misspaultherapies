## Understanding Grief

Grief is the natural response to loss — not just death, but any significant loss: relationship, job, health, identity, home, dream, or phase of life.

## Types of Grief

- **Anticipatory grief:** Grieving before the loss (terminal illness, impending divorce)
- **Acute grief:** The intense early response after a loss
- **Integrated grief:** The loss becomes part of your life story (not "over" but manageable)
- **Complicated grief:** When grief doesn't progress — stuck in intense pain for extended periods
- **Disenfranchised grief:** Losses not socially recognised (miscarriage, pet loss, estranged parent, relationship that "wasn't serious enough")
- **Ambiguous loss:** Person is physically present but mentally absent (dementia) or physically absent but status unknown (missing persons)

## The Dual Process Model

Healthy grieving oscillates between:
- **Loss-oriented:** Crying, remembering, feeling the pain
- **Restoration-oriented:** Building new routines, engaging in life, taking breaks from grief

Both are necessary. You don't need to grieve 24/7 to grieve "properly." Taking a break and laughing doesn't mean you've stopped caring.

## What Grief Looks Like

### Emotional
Sadness, anger, guilt, relief, numbness, anxiety, longing, irritability

### Physical
Fatigue, appetite changes, sleep disruption, chest tightness, weakened immunity

### Cognitive
Difficulty concentrating, confusion, disbelief, searching for meaning

### Behavioural
Social withdrawal, restlessness, crying, visiting places connected to the loss

## Grief in India

- Rituals provide structure (13-day mourning, annual ceremonies) — these can be healing
- But they can also rush grieving ("The rituals are done, move on")
- Widows face particular social restrictions and stigma
- Men expected to "be strong" and handle logistics, not emotions
- Grieving children often overlooked ("They don't understand")
- Collective grief is honoured; individual grief timeline is not

## What Helps

1. **There is no timeline.** Grief doesn't follow a schedule.
2. **Feel what you feel.** All emotions are valid — including anger and relief.
3. **Talk about the person.** Saying their name keeps them present.
4. **Maintain basic self-care.** Eat, sleep, hydrate — grief is physical.
5. **Accept help.** Let people cook, clean, sit with you.
6. **Create rituals.** Light a candle, visit a place, write a letter.
7. **Be patient with yourself.** Good days and bad days will alternate for a long time.

## When to Seek Grief Therapy

- Unable to function after 6+ months
- Suicidal thoughts
- Substance use to cope
- Complete avoidance of anything related to the loss
- Complicated grief (stuck, no movement)
- Guilt that won't resolve
- Pre-existing mental health conditions worsened by loss
