## What Resilience Is

Resilience isn't about being tough or unaffected. It's the ability to adapt and recover from stress, adversity, and trauma — not unchanged, but transformed. Resilient people still hurt; they just have tools to move through it.

## The 7 Pillars of Resilience

### 1. Emotional Awareness
Know what you're feeling. Name it. Process it. Don't suppress.

### 2. Impulse Control
Pause between stimulus and response. Choose rather than react.

### 3. Optimism (Realistic)
Not toxic positivity. Rather: "This is hard AND I can get through it."

### 4. Causal Analysis
Understand what went wrong without catastrophising or self-blame.

### 5. Empathy
Connect with others. Understand their perspective. Build support.

### 6. Self-Efficacy
Belief in your ability to influence outcomes. Build through small wins.

### 7. Reaching Out
Ask for help. Accept support. You don't have to do hard things alone.

## Building Resilience Daily

### Morning
- Set an intention: "Today I will respond rather than react"
- Recall one challenge you've already survived

### During Adversity
- "What is within my control here?"
- "What can I learn from this?"
- "Who can I turn to for support?"

### Evening
- Identify one thing you handled well today
- Name one thing you're grateful for despite difficulty
- Reflect: "What would I tell a friend in my situation?"

## Resilience After Specific Challenges

| Challenge | Resilience Practice |
|-----------|-------------------|
| Job loss | Focus on transferable skills; network |
| Relationship end | Grieve fully; rediscover identity |
| Health crisis | Accept what you can't control; advocate for yourself |
| Family conflict | Set boundaries; seek mediation |
| Financial stress | Create a plan; ask for help |

## What Undermines Resilience
- Isolation (trying to handle everything alone)
- Rumination (replaying problems without solving)
- Avoidance (pretending things are fine)
- Substance use (numbing instead of processing)
- Comparison (others seem to handle things better)
