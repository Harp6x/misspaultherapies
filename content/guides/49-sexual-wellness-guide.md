## The Connection Between Sex and Mental Health

Sexual wellness and mental health are deeply intertwined. Depression reduces libido, anxiety creates performance pressure, trauma affects intimacy, and relationship issues manifest in the bedroom.

## Common Concerns
- Low libido (desire issues)
- Performance anxiety (erectile difficulties, anorgasmia)
- Pain during sex (vaginismus, dyspareunia)
- Sexual trauma affecting current intimacy
- Mismatched desire levels in couples
- Body image issues affecting sexual confidence
- Pornography concerns
- Compulsive sexual behaviour

## Why India Doesn't Talk About It
- Sex education is minimal or absent
- Cultural taboo around discussing sex openly
- Arranged marriages with no pre-marital intimacy conversation
- Myths and misinformation from peers and internet
- Shame around sexual difficulties ("It means I'm not a real man/woman")
- Lack of trained sex-positive therapists

## What's Normal
- Desire fluctuates with stress, health, medication, life stage
- Not every sexual encounter needs to be "perfect"
- Desire styles differ (spontaneous vs responsive)
- Frequency varies greatly between couples
- Aging changes sexual response (normal, not pathological)

## When to Seek Help
- Persistent distress about sexual function
- Avoidance of intimacy affecting your relationship
- Sexual trauma affecting your ability to be intimate
- Compulsive sexual behaviour you can't control
- Significant disconnect between you and your partner
- Physical symptoms (pain, dysfunction) without medical cause

## What Therapy Looks Like
- Psychoeducation (often the biggest game-changer)
- Addressing underlying anxiety, depression, or trauma
- Communication skills for discussing sex with your partner
- Cognitive work on shame and performance pressure
- Sensate focus exercises (progressive intimacy without pressure)
- Collaborative work with medical professionals when needed
