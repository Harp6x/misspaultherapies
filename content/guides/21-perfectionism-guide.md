## Healthy Striving vs. Perfectionism

| Healthy Striving | Perfectionism |
|-----------------|---------------|
| "How can I improve?" | "I must be flawless" |
| Motivated by growth | Driven by fear of failure |
| Can celebrate progress | Only satisfied with perfection |
| Bounces back from mistakes | Crushed by mistakes |
| Self-worth is stable | Self-worth depends on achievement |

## Types of Perfectionism

1. **Self-oriented:** Impossibly high standards for yourself
2. **Other-oriented:** Unrealistic expectations of others
3. **Socially prescribed:** Believing others demand perfection from you

## The Perfectionism Trap

```
Set impossible standard → Fail to meet it (inevitable) → Self-criticism
→ Anxiety/shame → Try harder / Procrastinate → Repeat
```

## Where It Comes From in Indian Context
- Academic pressure ("98% isn't good enough — where did 2% go?")
- Comparison culture (cousins, neighbours' children)
- Conditional love ("We love you when you achieve")
- Fear of disappointing family
- Social media highlight reels
- Marriage market pressure ("Be perfect to find a good match")

## Breaking Free

### 1. Notice Perfectionist Thinking
- "If I can't do it perfectly, why bother?" → All-or-nothing
- "One mistake and everything is ruined" → Catastrophising
- "They'll think I'm incompetent" → Mind-reading

### 2. Challenge the Standard
- "Whose standard is this? Mine or someone else's?"
- "What would 'good enough' look like?"
- "Will this matter in 5 years?"

### 3. Practice Imperfection
- Send an email without re-reading 5 times
- Post something without editing it to death
- Leave one task at 80% and move on
- Share something you made before it's "ready"

### 4. Redefine Success
- Success = effort + learning, not flawlessness
- Done > perfect
- Progress > perfection

### 5. Self-Compassion
When you fail (and you will, because you're human):
- "This is a moment of difficulty"
- "Everyone fails sometimes"
- "May I be kind to myself right now"
