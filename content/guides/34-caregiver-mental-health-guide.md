## Who Is This For?

Anyone caring for: an ageing parent, a spouse with chronic illness, a child with special needs, a family member with mental illness or addiction, or anyone who depends on you for daily care.

## The Caregiver's Dilemma

You can't pour from an empty cup. Yet caregivers are expected to be endlessly available, patient, and selfless. The result: burnout, resentment, guilt, physical illness, and isolation.

## Stages of Caregiver Burnout

1. **Enthusiasm:** "I'll handle this. I love them."
2. **Stagnation:** "This is harder than I expected."
3. **Frustration:** "No one helps. I'm doing everything."
4. **Apathy:** "I don't care anymore." (This is burnout.)

## Warning Signs
- Exhaustion that rest doesn't fix
- Resentment toward the person you care for
- Neglecting your own health
- Social withdrawal
- Using substances to cope
- Losing empathy
- Fantasising about escape
- Physical symptoms (headaches, back pain, weakened immunity)

## What You Need

### Permission
- To feel frustrated, angry, or sad
- To take breaks without guilt
- To ask for help
- To not be perfect
- To have a life outside caregiving

### Practical Support
- Delegate: Who else can help? (siblings, friends, hired help)
- Respite care: Regular planned breaks (even 2 hours)
- Support groups: Others who understand (online or in-person)
- Financial planning: Explore insurance, government schemes

### Emotional Support
- Your own therapy (not optional — essential)
- Journaling to process complex emotions
- Mindfulness to manage moment-to-moment stress
- Boundaries with the care-receiver when needed

## Caregiver Self-Care Plan

| Frequency | Activity |
|-----------|----------|
| Daily | 15 minutes alone, one enjoyable activity |
| Weekly | Social connection outside caregiving role |
| Monthly | Half-day off (completely) |
| Quarterly | Full day or weekend break |

## Indian Context
- Caregiving expected as "duty" (seva, dharma)
- Women disproportionately bear caregiving burden
- Guilt about external help ("We should take care of our own")
- Nuclear family transition = fewer helpers
- Mental health of caregivers rarely discussed
