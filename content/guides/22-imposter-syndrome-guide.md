## What Is Imposter Syndrome?

The persistent belief that you're a fraud — that your success is due to luck, timing, or fooling others, and that you'll be "found out" any moment. It affects an estimated 70% of people at some point.

## The Five Types (Dr. Valerie Young)

1. **The Perfectionist:** Success is never enough. One mistake = failure.
2. **The Natural Genius:** If it doesn't come easily, you must not be smart enough.
3. **The Expert:** Feel you need to know everything before you can begin.
4. **The Soloist:** Asking for help means you're incompetent.
5. **The Superhero:** Must excel in every role simultaneously.

## Imposter Syndrome in India

- First-generation professionals (first in family to study abroad, enter corporate)
- Women in male-dominated fields
- Caste/class background creating "I don't belong here" feelings
- NRIs feeling like outsiders in both countries
- "Log kya kahenge" intensifying self-doubt
- Achievement-oriented families where nothing is ever enough

## Evidence-Based Strategies

### 1. The Evidence File
Create a document of concrete evidence of your competence:
- Positive feedback, awards, achievements
- Problems you've solved
- Skills you've developed
- People you've helped

Read it when imposter thoughts strike.

### 2. Reframe the Narrative
- "I got lucky" → "I was prepared when the opportunity came"
- "Anyone could do this" → "I bring unique perspective and skills"
- "I don't deserve this" → "I earned this through effort"

### 3. Normalise Not Knowing
Experts don't know everything — they know how to find answers. Not knowing something doesn't make you a fraud; it makes you human.

### 4. Talk About It
Imposter syndrome thrives in silence. Share your feelings with trusted peers — you'll be surprised how many feel the same.

### 5. Own Your Accomplishments
Practice saying "Thank you" without deflecting. When complimented: "Thank you, I worked hard on that" instead of "Oh, it was nothing."
