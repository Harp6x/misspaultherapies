## Procrastination Is Not Laziness

It's an emotional regulation problem. You procrastinate to avoid uncomfortable feelings associated with the task: anxiety, boredom, perfectionism, fear of failure, or overwhelm.

## The Procrastination Equation

```
Procrastination = (Task difficulty × Task aversiveness) / (Confidence × Urgency)
```

## Why You Procrastinate (Pick Your Pattern)

| Pattern | Root Cause | Fix |
|---------|-----------|-----|
| Perfectionism | Fear of not doing it perfectly | Set a "B-minus" standard |
| Overwhelm | Task feels too big | Break into 5-minute chunks |
| Boredom | Task isn't stimulating | Pair with music, body doubling |
| Fear of failure | Avoiding judgement | Reframe: "Done > perfect" |
| Decision paralysis | Too many options | Choose any option and start |
| Rebellion | Resisting external pressure | Find YOUR reason to do it |

## Core Techniques

### 1. The 5-Minute Rule
Commit to just 5 minutes. Starting is the hardest part — once you begin, momentum often carries you forward.

### 2. Temptation Bundling
Pair the unpleasant task with something enjoyable: listen to your favourite podcast while cleaning, eat your favourite snack while doing taxes.

### 3. Implementation Intentions
Instead of "I'll work on the report," say: "At 10 AM, I will open my laptop and write the introduction of the report at my desk."

### 4. Eat the Frog
Do the hardest task first thing in the morning when willpower is highest.

### 5. Accountability
Tell someone what you'll do and by when. External accountability increases follow-through by 95%.

### 6. Self-Compassion (Not Self-Criticism)
Research shows self-compassion after procrastination REDUCES future procrastination. Self-criticism increases it.
