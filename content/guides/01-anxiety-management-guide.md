## What Is Anxiety?

Anxiety is your body's natural response to perceived danger. It becomes a problem when:
- It shows up without a real threat
- It stays long after the threat has passed
- It interferes with daily functioning
- It causes avoidance of normal activities

**Important:** This guide is for education and self-awareness. It is not a substitute for professional assessment or treatment.

---

## Part 1: Understanding Your Anxiety

### The Anxiety Cycle

```
Trigger → Anxious Thought → Physical Sensation → Avoidance Behaviour → Short-term Relief → Long-term Anxiety Increases
```

### Common Types of Anxiety

| Type | Key Feature | Example |
|------|-------------|---------|
| Generalised Anxiety (GAD) | Excessive worry about many things | "What if I fail? What if something bad happens?" |
| Social Anxiety | Fear of judgement in social settings | Avoiding parties, presentations, phone calls |
| Panic Disorder | Sudden intense fear with physical symptoms | Heart racing, feeling like you're dying |
| Health Anxiety | Excessive worry about illness | Constantly checking symptoms online |
| Performance Anxiety | Fear of failure in specific situations | Exam stress, stage fright |

## Part 2: Body-Based Techniques

### 1. Diaphragmatic Breathing (4-7-8 Method)

This activates your parasympathetic nervous system — your body's built-in calm-down system.

**How to do it:**
1. Sit comfortably. Place one hand on your chest, one on your belly.
2. **Inhale** through your nose for **4 seconds** — feel your belly rise.
3. **Hold** for **7 seconds**.
4. **Exhale** slowly through your mouth for **8 seconds**.
5. Repeat 4–6 times.

**When:** Before a meeting, during a panic spiral, in bed when you can't sleep.

### 2. Progressive Muscle Relaxation (PMR)

Anxiety lives in the body. This technique teaches your muscles to release tension.

**How to do it:**
1. Start with your feet. Tense the muscles for 5 seconds.
2. Release and notice the difference for 10 seconds.
3. Move up: calves → thighs → abdomen → chest → hands → arms → shoulders → face.
4. End with 3 deep breaths.

**Time needed:** 10–15 minutes. Do daily for best results.

### 3. The Vagus Nerve Reset

The vagus nerve is the longest nerve in your body and controls your relaxation response.

**Quick resets:**
- Splash cold water on your face
- Hum or sing (vibrations stimulate the vagus nerve)
- Gargle water for 30 seconds
- Place a cold cloth on the back of your neck

## Part 3: Mind-Based Techniques

### 4. The Worry Decision Tree

When a worry appears, ask yourself:

```
Is this worry about something I can control?
├── YES → What is one small action I can take right now?
│         → Do it, then let the worry go.
└── NO  → Can I accept that this is outside my control?
          → Practice the phrase: "I notice I'm worrying about [X]. I choose to let this go for now."
```

### 5. Cognitive Restructuring (Thought Challenging)

| Step | Question | Example |
|------|----------|---------|
| 1. Identify the thought | "What am I thinking?" | "I'm going to embarrass myself at the meeting." |
| 2. Evidence for | "What supports this thought?" | "I stumbled over words last time." |
| 3. Evidence against | "What contradicts this thought?" | "I've done many meetings well. People said I was clear." |
| 4. Balanced thought | "What's a more realistic view?" | "I might be nervous, but I'm prepared and capable." |

### 6. The 5-4-3-2-1 Grounding Technique

When anxiety pulls you into the future, this brings you back to the present:

- **5** things you can SEE
- **4** things you can TOUCH
- **3** things you can HEAR
- **2** things you can SMELL
- **1** thing you can TASTE

## Part 4: Lifestyle Foundations

### Sleep
- Keep a consistent sleep schedule (even on weekends)
- No screens 30 minutes before bed
- Keep your room cool and dark
- If you can't sleep after 20 minutes, get up and do something calm, then return

### Movement
- 30 minutes of moderate exercise, 5 days a week
- Walking counts. Dancing counts. Playing with your dog counts.
- Exercise reduces cortisol and increases serotonin

### Nutrition
- Reduce caffeine (especially after 2pm)
- Stay hydrated (dehydration mimics anxiety symptoms)
- Eat regular meals (blood sugar drops trigger anxiety)

### Digital Boundaries
- Turn off non-essential notifications
- Set specific times for checking news/social media
- Unfollow accounts that increase your anxiety

## Part 5: When to Seek Professional Help

Consider therapy if:
- [ ] Anxiety persists for more than 2 weeks
- [ ] You're avoiding important activities
- [ ] Physical symptoms are disrupting sleep or appetite
- [ ] You're using alcohol, food, or other substances to cope
- [ ] You're having panic attacks
- [ ] It's affecting your work or relationships

**Remember:** Seeking help is not weakness — it's wisdom.
