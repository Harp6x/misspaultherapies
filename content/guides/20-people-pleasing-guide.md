# People-Pleasing — Understanding & Breaking the Pattern
### Ms Paul Therapies | @mspaultherapies

---

## What Is People-Pleasing?

People-pleasing is a pattern of prioritising others' needs, feelings, and approval over your own — often at significant personal cost. It's not kindness; it's a survival strategy rooted in fear.

## Where It Comes From

- **Childhood conditioning:** Love was conditional on being "good," obedient, or helpful
- **Indian cultural context:** Collectivist values emphasising sacrifice, duty, and "adjusting"
- **Trauma response (fawning):** Learned to keep others happy to stay safe
- **Low self-worth:** Believing your value comes from being useful to others
- **Fear of rejection:** "If I say no, they'll leave/be angry/stop loving me"

## Signs You're a People-Pleaser

- ☐ You say yes when you mean no
- ☐ You apologise excessively (even when not at fault)
- ☐ You avoid conflict at all costs
- ☐ You take responsibility for others' emotions
- ☐ You feel guilty when you prioritise yourself
- ☐ You change your opinions to match the room
- ☐ You overcommit and then feel resentful
- ☐ You can't identify what YOU want (only what others want from you)
- ☐ You feel exhausted by social interactions
- ☐ Your sense of self depends on others' approval

## The Hidden Cost

- Chronic resentment (giving without receiving)
- Burnout and exhaustion
- Loss of identity ("Who am I without my roles?")
- Anxiety (constant monitoring of others' moods)
- Attraction to narcissistic or controlling people
- Physical symptoms (headaches, GI issues, tension)

## Breaking the Pattern

### 1. Awareness
Notice when you're about to say yes out of obligation, guilt, or fear. Pause.

### 2. The Pause Practice
Before responding to a request: "Let me think about that and get back to you."
This single sentence changes everything.

### 3. Start with Small No's
- "I can't make it this time"
- "That doesn't work for me"
- "I'd prefer not to"

You don't need to explain, justify, or apologise.

### 4. Tolerate the Discomfort
Saying no will feel wrong at first. That discomfort is old programming, not truth. Sit with it. It passes.

### 5. Identify YOUR Needs
Daily: Ask yourself "What do I want? What do I need?" — and actually listen.

### 6. Redefine Kindness
Real kindness includes being kind to yourself. Self-sacrifice isn't noble when it destroys you.

## Affirmations for Recovery

- "I am allowed to say no without guilt"
- "My needs matter as much as anyone else's"
- "I am lovable even when I'm not useful"
- "Disappointing someone is not the same as hurting them"
- "I can be kind AND have boundaries"
