## Types of Eating Disorders

### Anorexia Nervosa
Restriction of food intake leading to significantly low body weight, intense fear of weight gain, distorted body image.

### Bulimia Nervosa
Cycles of binge eating followed by compensatory behaviours (purging, excessive exercise, fasting, laxative use).

### Binge Eating Disorder
Recurrent episodes of eating large amounts of food with a sense of loss of control, without regular compensatory behaviours.

### ARFID (Avoidant/Restrictive Food Intake Disorder)
Extremely picky eating based on sensory issues, fear of choking/vomiting, or lack of interest in food — NOT driven by body image.

### Orthorexia (Not Yet Official)
Obsession with "clean" or "healthy" eating to the point where it impairs health, social functioning, or quality of life.

## Eating Disorders in India

- Rising rapidly due to diet culture, social media, and Westernisation of beauty standards
- Colourism and "slim = beautiful" messaging starts early
- Wedding pressure ("lose weight before the shaadi")
- Family comments about body normalised ("You've become so fat")
- Vegetarian diets can mask restriction
- Less awareness = later detection = harder recovery

## Recovery Framework

### Phase 1: Medical Stabilisation
- Restore healthy eating patterns
- Address medical complications
- Nutritional rehabilitation with a dietitian
- Medication if needed (especially for co-occurring depression/anxiety)

### Phase 2: Psychological Treatment
- Identify function of the eating disorder (control, numbing, punishment, coping)
- Challenge distorted beliefs about food, weight, and worth
- Develop healthy coping mechanisms
- Process underlying trauma, perfectionism, or family dynamics
- Build body acceptance (not necessarily "love" — neutrality is a valid goal)

### Phase 3: Relapse Prevention
- Identify high-risk situations and triggers
- Build support network
- Develop a meal plan that feels sustainable
- Practice self-compassion during setbacks
- Gradual exposure to challenging foods and situations

## Key Principles

1. **Food is not the enemy** — it's medicine, nourishment, culture, and connection
2. **Recovery is not linear** — setbacks are part of the process
3. **Weight is not a measure of progress** — mental health improvements matter more
4. **You can't tell by looking** — eating disorders affect all body sizes
5. **Full recovery IS possible** — many people achieve lasting freedom from EDs

## For Families

- Don't comment on weight (yours or others')
- Don't restrict food groups without medical reason
- Model healthy relationships with food
- Don't use food as reward/punishment
- Educate yourself about eating disorders
- Seek family therapy if a loved one is affected
