## The Pain-Mental Health Loop

Chronic pain and mental health are deeply intertwined:
```
Pain → Limited activity → Frustration/depression → More pain perception
→ Less movement → More depression → Pain worsens
```

50% of people with chronic pain also have depression. Neither condition should be treated in isolation.

## Psychological Approaches to Pain Management

### 1. Pain Neuroscience Education
Understanding that pain is produced by the brain (not always reflecting tissue damage) can reduce pain intensity by 20-30%.

### 2. Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT)
- Accept pain as present without fighting it
- Commit to living a values-based life despite pain
- "I have pain AND I can still..."

### 3. Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction (MBSR)
- Body scan without trying to fix the pain
- Observing pain sensations without catastrophising
- Separating pain from suffering

### 4. Cognitive Restructuring
Challenge pain catastrophising:
- "This pain will never end" → "Pain fluctuates. I've had better days before."
- "I can't do anything" → "I can do less, but I can still do something"
- "My body is broken" → "My body is struggling, not broken"

### 5. Graded Activity
Gradually increasing activity based on time (not pain):
- Walk for 5 minutes regardless of pain
- Increase by 1 minute per week
- Activity is medicine, not punishment

## What Not to Say to Someone in Chronic Pain
- "It's all in your head"
- "You don't look sick"
- "Have you tried yoga?"
- "Just push through it"
- "At least it's not cancer"

## Indian Context
- Chronic pain normalised ("Everyone has back pain")
- Multiple doctor visits without psychological referral
- Ayurvedic/traditional remedies creating false hope or delay
- Family not understanding invisible illness
- Work expectations unchanged despite pain
