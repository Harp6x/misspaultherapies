## Understanding Anger

Anger is a **normal, healthy emotion.** It tells you that a boundary has been crossed, a need is unmet, or something feels unfair. The problem is never the anger itself — it's how we express it.

**Unhealthy anger expression:** Yelling, physical aggression, passive-aggression, suppression, sarcasm, blaming, silent treatment

**Healthy anger expression:** Assertive communication, setting boundaries, taking space, problem-solving

## Part 2: The Anger Iceberg

## Part 3: The Anger Thermometer

Rate your anger from 0 to 10 and know your strategies for each level:

| Level | Feeling | Strategy |
|-------|---------|----------|
| **0–2** (Calm) | Relaxed, at ease | Prevention: maintain self-care, boundaries |
| **3–4** (Annoyed) | Irritated, bothered | Notice it. Name it: "I'm getting irritated." Breathe. |
| **5–6** (Frustrated) | Tension building, harder to think clearly | Step away for 5 minutes. Splash cold water. Walk. |
| **7–8** (Angry) | Heart racing, raised voice, urge to react | Take a 20-minute break. Do NOT engage. Physical release (walk, exercise). |
| **9–10** (Rage) | Out of control, seeing red | Leave the situation immediately. Cool down for 30+ minutes. Do NOT make decisions. |

**Key rule:** The higher the number, the LESS you should talk or act. High anger = low logic.

## Part 4: Healthy Anger Expression

### The Assertive Formula

**"When [specific behaviour], I feel [emotion], because [impact]. I need [request]."**

| Aggressive ❌ | Assertive ✅ |
|--------------|-------------|
| "You're so inconsiderate!" | "When you come home late without telling me, I feel worried and disrespected, because I don't know if you're safe. I need you to send a text." |
| "You always dump work on me!" | "When I'm given extra tasks without discussion, I feel overwhelmed, because my workload is already full. I need us to discuss priorities." |
| "You never listen!" | "When I share something and you're on your phone, I feel unheard, because this matters to me. I need your full attention for 5 minutes." |

## Part 5: The Anger Timeout Protocol

When you feel yourself above a 6 on the anger thermometer:

1. **Signal:** Say calmly: "I need a break. I'll come back in 20 minutes." (NOT walking away in silence)
2. **Leave:** Go to a different room or outside
3. **Cool down:** Deep breathing, walk, cold water, listen to music
4. **Process:** Ask yourself: What am I really feeling? What do I need?
5. **Return:** Come back and communicate assertively

**Important:** The timeout is not avoidance. You MUST return to the conversation when calm.
