## What Is Mindfulness?

Mindfulness is paying attention to the present moment, on purpose, without judgement. It is not about emptying your mind — it's about noticing what's there.

**Benefits (backed by research):**
- Reduces anxiety and depression
- Improves focus and concentration
- Lowers blood pressure and cortisol
- Improves emotional regulation
- Better sleep quality

## Exercise 1: One-Minute Breathing

**Time:** 1 minute | **When:** Anytime, anywhere

1. Close your eyes (or soften your gaze)
2. Breathe normally — don't change anything
3. Simply **notice** each breath: the air entering your nose, your chest rising, the pause, the exhale
4. When your mind wanders (it will), gently return to the breath
5. Continue for 60 seconds

**Reflection:**
How many times did my mind wander? ___ (This is normal — noticing is the practice)

## Exercise 2: The Body Scan

**Time:** 5–10 minutes | **When:** Morning or before sleep

1. Lie down or sit comfortably
2. Starting at your toes, notice any sensations (warmth, tension, tingling, nothing)
3. Slowly move your attention upward: feet → ankles → calves → knees → thighs → hips → stomach → chest → hands → arms → shoulders → neck → face → top of head
4. Don't try to change anything — just notice
5. End with 3 deep breaths

## Exercise 3: Mindful Eating

**Time:** 5 minutes | **When:** Any meal or snack

Choose ONE bite of food (a piece of fruit, a nut, a spoonful of dal):

1. **Look** at it — notice colours, shapes, textures
2. **Smell** it — what do you notice?
3. **Place it in your mouth** — don't chew yet. Notice the texture, temperature
4. **Chew slowly** — notice flavours emerging. How does the taste change?
5. **Swallow** — feel it moving down your throat
6. **Pause** — notice the aftertaste, the absence

## Exercise 5: Mindful Walking

**Time:** 5–10 minutes | **When:** During a walk (commute, park, even inside your home)

1. Walk slowly and deliberately
2. Notice the sensation of each step: heel touching the ground, weight shifting, toes pushing off
3. Feel the air on your skin
4. Notice what you see without labelling — just shapes, colours, movement
5. If your mind wanders to thoughts, gently return attention to your feet

## Exercise 6: Leaves on a Stream

**Time:** 5 minutes | **When:** When thoughts are racing

1. Close your eyes and imagine sitting beside a gentle stream
2. Each time a thought comes, place it on a leaf and watch it float downstream
3. Don't push thoughts away or hold onto them — just observe them pass
4. If you get caught up in a thought, notice it and gently return to the stream
5. Even "I'm not doing this right" is a thought — put it on a leaf too

**How many leaves did I let go?** ___

## Exercise 7: The STOP Practice

**Time:** 30 seconds | **When:** Multiple times per day (set a reminder)

- **S** — Stop what you're doing
- **T** — Take one deep breath
- **O** — Observe: What am I thinking? Feeling? What sensations are in my body?
- **P** — Proceed with awareness

## Exercise 8: Gratitude Moment

**Time:** 2 minutes | **When:** Evening

Sit quietly and bring to mind:
1. **One person** you're grateful for today: _______________
2. **One moment** that was pleasant (even tiny): _______________
3. **One thing about your body** that worked well today: _______________

Allow yourself to FEEL the gratitude, not just think it. Notice warmth, a smile, a softening.

## Exercise 9: Loving-Kindness Meditation

**Time:** 5 minutes | **When:** When feeling disconnected, self-critical, or lonely

Silently repeat these phrases, first for yourself, then for others:

**For yourself:**
"May I be happy. May I be healthy. May I be safe. May I live with ease."

**For someone you love:**
"May [name] be happy. May they be healthy. May they be safe. May they live with ease."

**For someone neutral (a stranger, a shopkeeper):**
"May they be happy. May they be healthy. May they be safe. May they live with ease."

**For someone difficult (optional, only when ready):**
"May they be happy. May they be healthy. May they be safe. May they live with ease."

## Exercise 10: Mindful Listening

**Time:** 3 minutes | **When:** Anytime

1. Sit still and close your eyes
2. Listen to every sound around you — near and far
3. Don't label the sounds ("that's a car") — just hear them as pure sound
4. Notice sounds appearing and disappearing
5. Notice the silence between sounds
