## First: This Is Not Your Fault

Your parent's mental illness is not because of you. It's not your job to fix them. And you are allowed to have your own feelings about it — anger, sadness, confusion, embarrassment, love — all at the same time.

## What You Might Be Feeling

- **Confused:** "Why can't they just be normal?"
- **Angry:** "It's not fair. Other parents aren't like this."
- **Scared:** "Will I turn out like them?"
- **Guilty:** "If I were a better child, they'd be okay"
- **Parentified:** "I've been taking care of them since I was 10"
- **Invisible:** "Nobody asks how I'm doing"
- **Ashamed:** "I can't tell anyone about this"

**All of these are valid. Every single one.**

## Things to Remember

1. Mental illness is a medical condition — not a character flaw
2. Your parent loves you even when they can't show it the way you need
3. You are not responsible for their recovery
4. You ARE allowed to set boundaries with a sick parent
5. Getting help for yourself is not betrayal — it's survival
6. You are more than your family situation
7. Many people grow up with a mentally ill parent. You are not alone.

## What You Can Do

### For Them
- Be patient (when you can)
- Encourage them to stay in treatment
- Don't try to be their therapist
- Accept what you can't change

### For Yourself
- Talk to someone: therapist, school counsellor, trusted adult
- Join a support group (online or in-person)
- Maintain your own life: friends, hobbies, goals
- Set boundaries: "I love you AND I need to take care of myself"
- Learn about their condition (understanding reduces fear)
- Have a safety plan for crisis moments

## Will I Inherit It?

Genetics increase RISK, not certainty. Having a parent with depression doesn't mean you'll have it. And even if you do develop symptoms — you know what to look for, and you'll seek help earlier. That's your advantage.
