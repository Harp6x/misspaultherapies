## What You Need to Know First

Your partner's depression is not about you. It's not because of you. And you can't fix it. But you CAN be a safe, supportive presence while they get the help they need. This guide shows you how.

## What Depression Looks Like in a Partner

- They seem distant, disconnected, "not themselves"
- Loss of interest in things they used to enjoy (including intimacy)
- Sleeping too much or too little
- Irritability or short temper (depression isn't always sadness)
- Withdrawing from you and others
- "I'm fine" when they're clearly not
- Difficulty making decisions or concentrating
- Changes in appetite or weight

## What Helps

1. **Say:** "I'm here. You don't have to talk. But I'm not going anywhere."
2. **Be present** without trying to fix
3. **Don't take it personally** when they can't engage
4. **Gently encourage** therapy: "I think talking to someone could help. Want me to find someone?"
5. **Handle practical things** without being asked (meals, errands, appointments)
6. **Keep inviting** them to activities even if they say no — the invitation matters
7. **Celebrate small wins:** "You showered today. That's great." (They know it sounds small. It wasn't.)
8. **Educate yourself:** Understanding depression reduces frustration

## What Doesn't Help

- "Just think positive" / "Snap out of it"
- Getting frustrated when they can't function
- Comparing them to others: "Everyone deals with stress"
- Hovering or monitoring obsessively
- Making it about you: "Your depression is ruining our relationship"
- Trying to be their therapist
- Withdrawing because you feel helpless

## Taking Care of YOURSELF

You cannot pour from an empty cup. Caregiver burnout is real.

- Maintain your own friendships and hobbies
- Set boundaries: "I love you AND I need time for myself too"
- Seek your own therapy or support group
- Don't abandon your needs — resentment builds silently
- Remember: You're their partner, not their therapist

## When to Be Concerned

Seek immediate help if your partner:
- Talks about wanting to die or "not being here"
- Gives away possessions
- Suddenly seems calm after a long depression (may indicate a plan)
- Increases substance use
- Self-harms
