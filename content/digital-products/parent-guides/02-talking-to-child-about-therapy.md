## Why This Conversation Matters

How you introduce therapy shapes whether your child sees it as punishment or support. Get it right, and they'll be open. Get it wrong, and they'll resist — not because they don't need help, but because they're scared.

## Age-Appropriate Scripts

### Ages 5-8
"You know how we go to the doctor when our body hurts? A therapist is like a feelings doctor. They help when our feelings are too big or too confusing. You'll play games, draw, and talk. It's actually pretty fun."

### Ages 9-12
"Everyone has tough feelings sometimes — worry, sadness, anger. A therapist is a trained person who helps you understand those feelings and figure out what to do with them. It doesn't mean anything is wrong with you. It means you're brave enough to get help."

### Ages 13-15
"I've noticed things have been hard for you lately. A therapist is someone you can talk to honestly — someone who won't judge, won't tell me what you say, and is actually trained to help. It's not because you're broken. It's because you deserve support."

### Ages 16-18
"Therapy isn't for 'crazy' people. It's for anyone who wants to understand themselves better. Athletes have coaches. Students have tutors. Your mind deserves a professional too. I'll support whatever you decide, but I want you to try one session."

## Common Questions Kids Ask

**"Am I crazy?"**
"Not even close. Going to therapy means you're smart enough to take care of your mental health."

**"Will you know what I say?"**
"No. What you say is confidential — between you and the therapist. The only exception is if you're in danger."

**"Do I have to go?"**
"I'd like you to try one session. After that, we'll talk about how it felt."

**"Will my friends find out?"**
"Only if you tell them. It's your private information."

**"What if I don't like the therapist?"**
"That's okay. We'll find someone else. The fit matters."

## What NOT to Do
- Don't use therapy as a threat: "If you don't behave, I'll send you to a therapist"
- Don't share what the therapist tells you with relatives
- Don't expect overnight changes
- Don't ask "So what did you talk about?" after every session
- Don't cancel sessions because "they seem fine now"
