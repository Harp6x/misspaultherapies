## Page 1: You're Not Failing as a Parent

If you're reading this, you care. That already matters more than you think. This guide will help you understand what your teen is going through and how to support them — without pushing them away.

## Page 2: Warning Signs to Watch For

### Emotional
- Persistent sadness or irritability (2+ weeks)
- Crying more than usual
- Expressing hopelessness: "What's the point?"
- Anger outbursts that seem disproportionate

### Behavioural
- Withdrawal from friends and family
- Loss of interest in hobbies
- Sleep changes (too much or too little)
- Appetite changes
- Decline in academic performance
- Increased phone/screen dependence

### Concerning
- Self-harm (cuts, burns, scratches)
- Talk about death or suicide (even "jokingly")
- Substance use
- Giving away possessions
- Sudden calm after a period of depression (can indicate a plan)

**If you see these, seek professional help immediately.**

## Page 3: What NOT to Say

| Don't Say | Why It Hurts | Say Instead |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| "You have nothing to be depressed about" | Invalidates their experience | "Tell me what's going on for you" |
| "When I was your age..." | Dismisses their reality | "Things are different now. Help me understand" |
| "Stop being dramatic" | Shames their emotions | "I can see you're really hurting" |
| "Just focus on studies" | Reduces them to performance | "Your wellbeing matters more than grades" |
| "We don't need therapy" | Blocks access to help | "Let's explore what might help you" |

## Page 4: What TO Say

- "I've noticed you seem [sad/withdrawn/angry]. I'm here when you're ready."
- "You don't have to talk now. But I want you to know I'm not going anywhere."
- "It's okay to not be okay."
- "I love you no matter what."
- "Would it help to talk to someone outside the family?"

**Key: Ask. Listen. Don't fix. Validate.**

## Page 5: How to Bring Up Therapy

- Normalise it: "Therapy is like having a coach for your mind"
- Share: "Even adults go to therapy. I'd go too."
- Give choice: "Would you prefer online or in-person?"
- Remove pressure: "Try one session. If you don't like it, we'll figure something else out."
- Respect confidentiality: "What you say to the therapist stays between you and them."

## Page 6: How to Support Without Hovering

- Check in gently (not interrogating)
- Respect their space while being available
- Don't read their journal or phone (builds trust, not surveillance)
- Model emotional health yourself
- Family time without agenda (walks, meals, drives)
- Be patient. Healing isn't linear.

Parenting a struggling teen is one of the hardest things in the world. Please take care of yourself too. You can't pour from an empty cup. If you need support, reach out. You deserve it as much as they do.
