Hi,

Diwali, Navratri, Dussehra, Eid, Christmas — the festive season is here. And while it's supposed to be joyful, for many people it's also:

- Exhausting (social obligations, hosting, shopping, cooking)
- Triggering (family dynamics, comparisons, invasive questions)
- Lonely (for those away from home, grieving, or isolated)
- Financially stressful (gifts, clothes, expectations)
- Overwhelming (sensory overload — noise, crowds, firecrackers)

**You're allowed to feel both gratitude AND exhaustion.** They can coexist.

### The Festive Season Survival Kit

**Before gatherings:**
- Set an intention: "I will enjoy what I can and leave when I need to"
- Decide your boundaries in advance (what questions you won't engage with, how long you'll stay)
- Have an exit strategy (drive yourself, have a "phone call excuse" ready)

**During gatherings:**
- Take bathroom breaks as mini-resets (breathe, ground yourself)
- You don't owe anyone explanations about your life choices (marriage, career, weight, children)
- Scripts for invasive questions:
  - "Shaadi kab?" → "When the time is right. Tell me about YOUR life!"
  - "You've gained/lost weight" → "I'm happy with where I am. How are you doing?"
  - "When will you have kids?" → "That's a personal topic. Let's talk about something else."

**After gatherings:**
- Decompress. Don't schedule back-to-back social events.
- Journaling prompt: "What drained me today? What filled me up?"
- It's okay to skip some events. FOMO is temporary. Burnout is not.

### For Those Grieving During Festivities

If you've lost someone and the celebrations feel hollow — that's grief. It's valid. You don't have to pretend to be happy.

- Light a diya for them
- Share a memory with someone who knew them
- Give yourself permission to step away when it's too much
- Grief doesn't follow a calendar
