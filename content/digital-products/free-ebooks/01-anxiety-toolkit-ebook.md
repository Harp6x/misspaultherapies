## Page 4: Technique 1 — Box Breathing (4-4-4-4)

**When to use:** Panic rising, before presentations, can't sleep

1. Breathe IN for 4 seconds
2. HOLD for 4 seconds
3. Breathe OUT for 4 seconds
4. HOLD for 4 seconds
5. Repeat 4 cycles

**Why it works:** Activates your parasympathetic nervous system (the "rest and digest" mode). Navy SEALs use this.

**Pro tip:** Place one hand on your chest, one on your belly. Only the belly hand should move.

---

## Page 5: Technique 2 — The 5-4-3-2-1 Grounding

**When to use:** Feeling detached, dissociating, spiraling

Name:
- **5** things you can SEE
- **4** things you can HEAR
- **3** things you can TOUCH
- **2** things you can SMELL
- **1** thing you can TASTE

**Why it works:** Forces your brain out of "threat mode" and into the present moment.

---

## Page 6: Technique 3 — The Thought Challenge

**When to use:** Catastrophic thinking, "what if" loops

Ask yourself:
1. What am I afraid will happen?
2. What's the evidence FOR this thought?
3. What's the evidence AGAINST it?
4. What would I tell a friend thinking this?
5. What's the MOST LIKELY outcome?

**Why it works:** CBT's gold standard — breaks the cycle of automatic negative thoughts.

---

## Page 7: Technique 4 — The Worry Window

**When to use:** Constant background worry all day

Schedule 15 minutes daily as your "worry time." When a worry pops up outside that window, write it down and say: "I'll worry about this at 6pm." During your worry window, worry intentionally. When the timer goes off, stop.

**Why it works:** 80% of worries resolve themselves before worry time arrives.

---

## Page 8: Technique 5 — Cold Water Dive Response

**When to use:** Intense panic, rage, emotional overwhelm (8+/10)

Hold your breath and submerge your face in cold water for 30 seconds. OR: Hold an ice pack against your cheeks and forehead.

**Why it works:** Triggers the mammalian dive reflex — instantly slows heart rate by up to 25%. DBT's fastest skill.

---

## Page 9: Technique 6 — The STOP Skill

**When to use:** About to react impulsively

**S** — Stop. Don't move.
**T** — Take a breath. One deep one.
**O** — Observe. What am I feeling? What do I need?
**P** — Proceed mindfully. Choose a wise response.

---

## Page 10: Technique 7 — Progressive Muscle Relaxation (Mini)

**When to use:** Body tension, jaw clenching, can't relax

Tense each muscle group for 5 seconds, then release for 10:
1. Fists → release
2. Shoulders to ears → release
3. Scrunch face → release
4. Tighten stomach → release
5. Curl toes → release

Total time: 3 minutes. The contrast between tension and release teaches your body what "relaxed" feels like.

---

## Page 11: Techniques 8-10 — Quick Hits

**8. Name It to Tame It**
Say: "I notice I'm feeling anxious." Naming an emotion reduces amygdala activation by 50% (UCLA research).

**9. The 3-3-3 Rule**
Name 3 things you see, 3 sounds you hear, move 3 body parts. Takes 30 seconds.

**10. Opposite Action**
Anxiety says "avoid." Do the opposite — approach (gently). Anxiety shrinks when you prove it wrong.
