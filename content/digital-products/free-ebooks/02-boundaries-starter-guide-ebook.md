## Page 3: What Are Boundaries, Really?

Boundaries aren't walls. They're doors with locks — YOU decide who gets a key.

**A boundary is:** A clear statement of what you will and won't accept.
**A boundary is NOT:** Controlling someone else's behaviour.

| What boundaries ARE | What they AREN'T |
|--------------------|--------------------|
| "I won't answer calls after 10pm" | "You can't call me" |
| "I need 30 minutes alone after work" | "Leave me alone forever" |
| "I'm not comfortable discussing my weight" | "You're rude for asking" |

## Page 4: The 6 Types of Boundaries

1. **Physical** — Personal space, touch, privacy
2. **Emotional** — Protecting your feelings, not absorbing others'
3. **Time** — How you spend your hours, saying no to overcommitment
4. **Digital** — Screen time, social media, who can contact you when
5. **Material** — Money, possessions, lending
6. **Intellectual** — Your opinions, thoughts, beliefs being respected

## Page 5: Why Boundaries Feel So Hard (Especially in India)

- **Family enmeshment:** "We don't have boundaries in our family" = "We don't have privacy"
- **Cultural guilt:** Saying no to elders = disrespect
- **Gender expectations:** Women should adjust, sacrifice, accommodate
- **Fear of abandonment:** "If I set limits, they'll leave"

**The truth:** People who leave when you set boundaries were benefiting from you having none.

## Page 6: The Boundary Formula

**"I [state your boundary]. [Consequence if crossed]."**

Examples:
- "I love you, AND I'm not able to lend money right now."
- "I'd prefer not to discuss my marriage plans. Let's talk about something else."
- "I need to leave by 9pm. If plans start later, I won't be able to join."

Notice: No apology. No explanation. No negotiation.

## Page 7: 15 Ready-to-Use Boundary Scripts

### With Parents
1. "I value your opinion. I've already made my decision on this."
2. "I'd rather you didn't comment on my body/weight."
3. "I'll visit when I'm able to, not every weekend."

### At Work
4. "I can take this on if we reprioritise [X]. Which should I drop?"
5. "I'll respond to that during work hours tomorrow."
6. "I need to protect my lunch break today."

### With Partner
7. "I need 20 minutes to decompress before we talk about this."
8. "I don't accept being yelled at. I'll continue this conversation when it's calm."
9. "I need us to make decisions together, not for you to decide for both of us."

### With Friends
10. "I can't make it tonight. No reason — just need to rest."
11. "I'd rather not be the person everyone vents to without asking first."
12. "I love you AND I can't be your only support. Have you considered talking to a therapist?"

### With Extended Family/Society
13. "That's a personal question I'd rather not answer."
14. "My timeline for [marriage/kids/career] is my own."
15. "I appreciate the concern. I've got it handled."

## Page 8: The Guilt Will Come — Here's How to Handle It

Guilt after setting a boundary is normal. It does NOT mean you did something wrong.

**Sit with it.** Don't undo the boundary because of the feeling.
**Remind yourself:** "Guilt is a habit from an old system. I'm building a new one."
**Journal:** "I set a boundary because ____. I chose myself because ____."

---

## Page 9: What If They Get Angry?

They might. Especially people used to you having no limits.

**Their anger is their boundary problem, not yours.**

Responses:
- "I understand you're upset. My boundary still stands."
- "I hear you. This is what I need."
- Silence. (You don't owe an explanation.)
