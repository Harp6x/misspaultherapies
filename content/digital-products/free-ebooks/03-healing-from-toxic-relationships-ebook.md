## Page 3: Red Flags You Probably Ignored (Because Love)

### Emotional Abuse
- Criticism disguised as "jokes"
- Silent treatment as punishment
- Constant comparison to others
- Making you feel like nothing you do is enough
- "I'm the only one who really loves you"

### Control
- Monitoring your phone/social media
- Deciding who you can see
- Financial control
- Guilt-tripping you for having a life outside them

### Gaslighting
- "That never happened"
- "You're too sensitive"
- "You're remembering it wrong"
- "Nobody else would put up with you"

## Page 4: The Trauma Bond — Why You Stayed

It's not weakness. It's brain chemistry.

**Intermittent reinforcement** — unpredictable kindness mixed with cruelty creates the strongest emotional bonds (same mechanism as gambling addiction).

The cycle: **Tension → Explosion → Remorse/Love-bombing → Calm → Repeat**

Each "love-bombing" phase floods your brain with dopamine. You're literally addicted to the highs because the lows are so devastating.

**You stayed because your brain was hijacked, not because you're stupid.**

## Page 5: The 5 Stages of Leaving

1. **Denial** — "It's not that bad. Every couple fights."
2. **Bargaining** — "If I change, they'll change."
3. **Anger** — "How dare they treat me this way?"
4. **Grief** — "I'm mourning the person I thought they were."
5. **Acceptance** — "This was real. And I deserve better."

You'll cycle through these. It's not linear. That's okay.

## Page 6: What Recovery Actually Looks Like

### Month 1-3: Survival Mode
- Go no-contact or strict low-contact
- Build a safety plan
- Lean on your support system
- Expect grief, anger, and relief — all at once

### Month 3-6: Processing
- Start therapy (trauma-informed)
- Journal about what happened
- Begin to see the patterns
- Grieve the fantasy, not the reality

### Month 6-12+: Rebuilding
- Rediscover who you are outside the relationship
- Rebuild self-trust
- Set boundaries in all relationships
- Learn your attachment patterns

## Page 8: Rebuilding Self-Trust

The hardest part of toxic relationships isn't what they did. It's losing trust in yourself.

"How did I not see it?" → Because manipulation is designed to be invisible.
"Why did I stay?" → Because you loved the potential, not the pattern.
"Can I trust my judgment again?" → Yes. With practice.

### Self-Trust Rebuilding Exercise
Each day, make ONE small decision and honour it:
- "I'll leave the party by 10pm" → Leave at 10pm
- "I'll say no to this favour" → Say no
- "I'll eat when I'm hungry" → Eat

Stack these. Your self-trust grows one kept promise at a time.

## Page 9: No-Contact Survival Guide

### Rules
- Block on all platforms (yes, all of them)
- Delete their number (screenshot and give to a friend if needed for safety)
- Ask mutual friends not to relay information
- Write a "why I left" letter to yourself — read it when you want to go back

### When You Want to Break No-Contact
1. Read your "why I left" letter
2. Call your emergency contact instead
3. Wait 24 hours. The urge will pass.
4. Journal: "What do I actually miss — them, or the fantasy?"

## Page 10: When to Seek Professional Help

You need a therapist if:
- Flashbacks or nightmares about the relationship
- You've started a new relationship but can't trust them
- Anxiety, depression, or PTSD symptoms
- You keep choosing the same type of partner
- You feel "stuck" and can't move forward alone

**There is no shame in needing help. There is only courage.**
