## Why Festivals Can Be Hard

- **Social overload** — back-to-back events with no recovery time
- **Family dynamics** — invasive questions, comparisons, old conflicts resurface
- **Financial pressure** — gifts, clothes, sweets, decorations
- **Grief amplifier** — missing those who aren't here anymore
- **Sensory overwhelm** — noise (firecrackers), crowds, lights
- **Body image triggers** — shopping for clothes, comments about appearance
- **FOMO** — seeing others' celebrations online

## The Festive Survival Plan

### Boundaries Script Pack

| They Say | You Say |
|----------|---------|
| "Shaadi kab?" | "When the time is right! What about you — how's YOUR year been?" |
| "You've put on weight" | "I'm feeling great. Let's talk about something more fun." |
| "Still no promotion?" | "I'm happy with where I am. Tell me about your work." |
| "When are you having kids?" | "That's between us. I'd rather hear about your family." |
| "You should dress more traditionally" | "I love what I'm wearing! You look great too." |

### Daily Festival Checklist
- [ ] Slept 7+ hours last night
- [ ] Ate a proper meal (not just mithai)
- [ ] Took 15 minutes alone today
- [ ] Said no to at least one thing I didn't want to do
- [ ] Connected with one person who makes me feel good
- [ ] Moved my body (even 10 minutes)
- [ ] Set a spending limit and stuck to it

### For Those Grieving During Festivals
- Light a diya in their memory
- Tell a story about them to someone who'll listen
- It's okay to step away from celebrations
- Grief and joy CAN coexist — you don't have to choose
- Give yourself permission to feel whatever comes up

### Post-Festival Recovery
- Schedule 1 full day of nothing after major events
- Journaling prompt: "What drained me? What filled me up?"
- Digital detox for 24 hours (stop comparing your celebration to others')
- Gentle movement — walk, stretch, yoga
- Return to your routine gradually
