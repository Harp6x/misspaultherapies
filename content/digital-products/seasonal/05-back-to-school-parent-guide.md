## Signs Your Child May Be Struggling

### Emotional
- [ ] Increased irritability or crying
- [ ] Excessive worry about school
- [ ] Withdrawal from friends or activities
- [ ] Clinginess (especially in younger children)
- [ ] Saying "I hate school" persistently

### Physical
- [ ] Stomach aches or headaches before school
- [ ] Sleep changes (trouble falling asleep or oversleeping)
- [ ] Appetite changes
- [ ] Bedwetting (if previously dry)
- [ ] Fatigue

### Behavioural
- [ ] Refusing to go to school
- [ ] Declining grades
- [ ] Avoiding previously enjoyed activities
- [ ] Increased screen time / withdrawal
- [ ] Acting out or aggression

**If 3+ signs persist for 2+ weeks, consider professional support.**

## Conversation Starters (By Age)

### Ages 5–8
- "What was the best part of your day? What was the worst?"
- "Who did you play with today? What did you play?"
- "Did anything make you feel sad or scared today?"
- "If you could change one thing about school, what would it be?"

### Ages 9–12
- "What's something funny that happened today?"
- "Is there anything at school that's bothering you?"
- "How are things with your friends?"
- "Do you feel like your teacher understands you?"

### Ages 13–17
- "I noticed you seem quiet/stressed. Want to talk or just hang out?"
- "What's something you're looking forward to this week?"
- "Is there anything you need help with that you haven't asked about?"
- "I'm here whenever you're ready to talk. No pressure."

**Pro tip:** Don't ask questions in the car facing them. Kids talk more when you're side by side (driving, walking, cooking together).

## Homework Without the War

1. **Create a routine** (same time, same place — not right after school; give them a break first)
2. **Be available, not hovering** ("I'm in the kitchen if you need help")
3. **Focus on effort, not grades** ("I noticed you worked really hard on that")
4. **Don't do it for them** (struggle is part of learning)
5. **If homework consistently takes 2+ hours, talk to the teacher**

## Academic Pressure: What NOT to Say

| Don't Say | Try Instead |
|-----------|-------------|
| "Your cousin scored 95%" | "How do YOU feel about your result?" |
| "You need to study harder" | "What's making this subject difficult?" |
| "No playing until you finish" | "Let's make a plan for study and play" |
| "This will ruin your future" | "One test does not define you" |
| "I'm disappointed in you" | "I believe in you. How can I help?" |

## Building Resilience

- Let them fail safely (not every problem needs your rescue)
- Teach problem-solving ("What do you think you could try?")
- Model emotional regulation (let them see you handle stress healthily)
- Validate feelings before fixing ("That sounds really frustrating")
- Celebrate effort and character, not just results
