# Slide Deck: Supporting Your Teen's Mental Health — Parent Talk
### Ms Paul Therapies | @mspaultherapies

**Use:** School parent-teacher meeting, parenting workshop, community event
**Format:** 30 slides, 40-50 min
**Audience:** Parents of teenagers (13-18)

---

## Slide Outline

### Opening (Slides 1-4)
1. Title: "They're Not Just Being Dramatic" — Understanding Your Teenager's Mental Health
2. About Aishani Paul
3. Poll: "How confident are you in talking to your teen about mental health?" (1-10)
4. Agenda

### Part 1: What's Happening in Their Brain (Slides 5-9)
5. The teenage brain: Prefrontal cortex still developing until age 25
6. Emotional brain (amygdala) runs the show — they FEEL before they THINK
7. This isn't defiance — it's development
8. Hormones, identity, social hierarchy — all at once
9. The pressure cooker: academics + social media + peer + family

### Part 2: Warning Signs (Slides 10-15)
10. Mood changes: Irritability, withdrawal, sadness lasting 2+ weeks
11. Behavioural changes: Sleep, appetite, grades, friend group
12. Social withdrawal: Not the same as introversion
13. Self-harm & substance use: What to look for
14. Academic changes: Sudden drop, loss of interest
15. The difference between "normal teen" and "teen in crisis"

### Part 3: What NOT to Say (Slides 16-19)
16. "Other kids don't have these problems" → They do. They're just hiding it.
17. "You have nothing to be stressed about" → Their world IS their whole world.
18. "Stop being so dramatic" → Their feelings are real, even if you don't understand.
19. "We never needed therapy" → Different generation, different challenges.

### Part 4: What TO Say & Do (Slides 20-25)
20. "I've noticed you seem ____. I'm here when you're ready to talk."
21. Listen to understand, not to fix
22. Validate before advising: "That sounds really hard"
23. Create safe moments (car rides, walks, late-night chats > formal sit-downs)
24. Normalise emotions: Share YOUR struggles (age-appropriately)
25. Respect their privacy while maintaining safety

### Part 5: When to Seek Help (Slides 26-28)
26. When to worry: Persistent symptoms, self-harm, suicidal talk, substance use
27. How to bring up therapy: "A therapist is like a coach for your mind"
28. Finding a teen-friendly therapist: What to look for

### Closing (Slides 29-30)
29. Key takeaways + resource handout (QR code)
30. Q&A + Thank you
