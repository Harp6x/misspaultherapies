## Email 1: Why You Keep Having the Same Fight
**Day 0 | Subject:** Why you keep having the same fight
**Preview:** It's not about the dishes. It's never about the dishes.

---

Hi,

Every couple has "the fight." The one that keeps coming back. Maybe it's about:
- Housework
- Money
- In-laws
- Screen time
- "You never listen"

But here's what 10+ years of relationship research tells us: **the topic is rarely the real issue.**

The real issue is almost always one of three things:
1. **"Do you see me?"** (Am I important to you?)
2. **"Can I count on you?"** (Are you there when I need you?)
3. **"Do you respect me?"** (Do you value my perspective?)

The dishes aren't about the dishes. They're about: "Do you care enough about our shared life to contribute equally?"

The phone isn't about the phone. It's about: "Am I less interesting to you than your screen?"

**This week, during your next disagreement, pause and ask yourself:** "What am I REALLY upset about underneath this?"

You might be surprised.

Over the next few emails, I'll share the exact communication techniques I teach couples in therapy.

With warmth,
**Aishani**

## Email 2: The 3-Minute Conversation That Changes Everything
**Day 2 | Subject:** The 3-minute conversation that changes everything
**Preview:** Most couples skip this. Don't.

---

Hi,

Dr. John Gottman studied thousands of couples and found that **the first 3 minutes of a conversation predict its outcome 96% of the time.**

Start with blame → the conversation escalates.
Start with understanding → the conversation resolves.

**The Soft Startup Formula:**

Instead of: "You never help with anything." (blame, generalisation)
Try: "I feel overwhelmed with the housework lately. Could we figure out how to share it?" (feeling + request)

**The structure:**
"I feel [emotion] about [specific situation]. I need [clear request]."

**More examples:**

❌ "You're always on your phone!"
✅ "I feel disconnected when we're both on screens at dinner. Can we try phone-free meals?"

❌ "You don't care about my feelings."
✅ "I feel unheard when I share something and the topic changes. I need you to acknowledge what I said."

❌ "Why don't you ever plan anything?"
✅ "I'd love it if you planned a date for us sometime. It would make me feel prioritised."

**Your homework:** The next time you want to bring something up with your partner, take 30 seconds to formulate it using the formula before you speak.

The way you start changes everything.
**Aishani**

## Email 3: How to Listen So Your Partner Feels Heard
**Day 4 | Subject:** How to listen so your partner actually feels heard
**Preview:** Hint: it's not about fixing

---

Hi,

The biggest communication mistake in relationships isn't saying the wrong thing. It's **listening to respond instead of listening to understand.**

When your partner shares something, your brain immediately starts:
- Planning a defence
- Thinking of a counter-argument
- Trying to "fix" the problem
- Waiting for them to finish so you can speak

None of that is listening.

**Real listening looks like:**

**1. Put everything down.** Phone away. TV off. Face them.

**2. Let them finish.** Don't interrupt. Even if you disagree. Even if it takes longer than you want.

**3. Paraphrase what you heard.** "What I'm hearing is that you feel [X] when [Y] happens. Is that right?"

**4. Validate (even if you disagree).** "I can understand why you'd feel that way." Validation ≠ agreement. It means "your feelings make sense."

**5. Ask: "What do you need from me?"** Sometimes they want help. Sometimes they just want to be heard. Don't assume — ask.

**The magic sentence:** "Tell me more about that."

These four words communicate: I'm interested. I'm not judging. I want to understand you.

Try it tonight. The results will surprise you.
**Aishani**

## Email 4: Fighting Fair — The Rules
**Day 7 | Subject:** Rules for fighting fair (agree on these tonight)
**Preview:** Every healthy couple has ground rules for conflict

---

Hi,

Conflict is NORMAL. Every healthy couple disagrees. The question isn't whether you fight — it's HOW you fight.

**The Fair Fight Agreement — print this and put it on your fridge:**

**We agree that during disagreements:**

1. ✅ We attack the PROBLEM, not each other
2. ✅ We use "I" statements, not "You" accusations
3. ✅ Either person can call a timeout (20 minutes, then we come back)
4. ✅ We stay on the current issue (no bringing up the past)
5. ✅ We repair after — apology, hug, acknowledgement

**We agree NOT to:**

1. ❌ Raise our voices
2. ❌ Use "always" or "never" ("You ALWAYS..." / "You NEVER...")
3. ❌ Give the silent treatment
4. ❌ Involve family members in our arguments
5. ❌ Go to bed without at least acknowledging the conflict
6. ❌ Use contempt (eye-rolling, sarcasm, mockery)
7. ❌ Threaten the relationship ("Maybe we should just break up")

**The Repair Attempt:**
When things escalate, the person who notices first says: "Can we start over? I don't want to fight like this."

Research shows that couples who make repair attempts — even clumsy ones — are significantly more likely to stay together and stay happy.

**Tonight:** Sit with your partner and go through this list together. Agree on YOUR version of these rules. Write them down.

Rules don't restrict love. They protect it.
**Aishani**

## Email 5: When to Consider Couples Therapy
**Day 10 | Subject:** When two people who love each other need a third
**Preview:** Couples therapy isn't a last resort. It's a smart investment.

---

Hi,

There's a myth that couples therapy is what you do when the relationship is dying. Actually, the best time for couples therapy is when the relationship is **struggling but both people still care.**

**Consider couples therapy if:**
- The same arguments keep repeating with no resolution
- You feel more like roommates than partners
- Communication has broken down (talking at each other, not with each other)
- There's been a breach of trust
- External stress (money, in-laws, parenting) is creating distance
- One or both of you feels unheard, unloved, or unimportant
- Intimacy (emotional or physical) has significantly declined
- You're thinking about separation but haven't decided

**What couples therapy is NOT:**
- A referee who declares a winner
- A lecture about what you're doing wrong
- One partner's "fix" for the other
- A guarantee of staying together (sometimes it helps people separate well)

**What it IS:**
- A safe space to say things that are hard to say at home
- A skilled translator between two people who love each other but can't seem to connect
- A toolkit of evidence-based strategies you can use for the rest of your life
- An investment in your family's foundation

**My approach:**
I work with couples using Gottman Method principles and Emotionally Focused Therapy (EFT). Both have strong research behind them. I don't take sides — I'm on the relationship's side.
