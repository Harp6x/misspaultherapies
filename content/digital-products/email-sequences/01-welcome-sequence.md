## Email 2: The One Thing Nobody Tells You
**Send:** Day 2
**Subject:** The one thing nobody tells you about mental health
**Preview text:** I learned this the hard way

---

Hi,

Here's something I wish someone had told me years ago:

**Mental health is not a destination. It's a daily practice.**

We treat it like a switch — you're either "fine" or "not fine." But the truth is, mental health is more like physical fitness. You don't go to the gym once and declare yourself fit forever.

Some days are easier. Some are harder. And that's not failure — that's being human.

Here are three things I've learned from working with hundreds of clients:

**1. The strongest people I know are the ones who ask for help.**
Not the ones who suffer in silence and "handle it."

**2. Small daily habits matter more than big occasional gestures.**
5 minutes of breathing > one expensive wellness retreat you do once a year.

**3. Awareness is already halfway to healing.**
The fact that you downloaded that guide and you're reading this email? That's awareness. That counts.

This week, try this: at the end of each day, ask yourself one question:

*"How am I, really?"*

Not "I'm fine." Really sit with it. You might be surprised what comes up.

Talk soon,
**Aishani**

## Email 3: 3 Signs You Need More Than Self-Care
**Send:** Day 4
**Subject:** 3 signs you might need more than "just self-care"
**Preview text:** Face masks and journaling can't fix everything

---

Hi,

I love self-care. I recommend it to every client.

But I also need to be honest: **self-care has limits.**

There are times when bubble baths, journaling, and "taking a break" aren't enough. And that's not because you're doing it wrong — it's because some things need professional support.

**Here are 3 signs it might be time for something more:**

**1. The same feelings keep coming back.**
You feel better for a while, then slide back. The sadness, anxiety, or anger keeps returning like clockwork. Self-care manages symptoms; therapy addresses root causes.

**2. You're functioning but not flourishing.**
You go to work, pay your bills, smile at people — but inside, you feel flat. Like you're going through the motions. This "high-functioning" struggle is one of the most common things I see.

**3. You catch yourself saying "it's not that bad."**
If you have to convince yourself you're fine, you might not be. You don't need to hit rock bottom to deserve support.

Here's the thing: therapy isn't just for crises. It's for anyone who wants to understand themselves better and live with more clarity and peace.

If any of this resonated, I have a [therapy readiness checklist](https://mspaultherapies.com/guide) that might help you decide.

Be gentle with yourself.
**Aishani**

## Email 4: Your 5-Minute Anxiety Reset
**Send:** Day 7
**Subject:** Your 5-minute anxiety reset (try it right now)
**Preview text:** Works in meetings, at home, even in traffic

---

Hi,

I want to give you something you can use right now — not "someday" but literally in the next 5 minutes.

**The 5-Minute Anxiety Reset:**

**Minute 1: Ground yourself.**
Feel your feet on the floor. Press them down. Notice the pressure, the temperature. You're here. You're safe.

**Minute 2: Breathe with a count.**
Inhale for 4 seconds. Hold for 4. Exhale for 6. The longer exhale activates your parasympathetic nervous system — your body's built-in calm button. Repeat 4 times.

**Minute 3: Name it.**
Say (out loud or silently): "I notice I'm feeling anxious." Naming an emotion reduces its intensity by up to 50% — this is backed by fMRI brain studies.

**Minute 4: Challenge one thought.**
What's the thought driving your anxiety? Ask yourself: "Is this a fact, or is this a worry?" Most of the time, it's a worry dressed up as a fact.

**Minute 5: Choose one action.**
What is ONE thing you can do right now that moves you forward? Send that email. Drink water. Stand up and stretch. Take the smallest possible step.

**Bookmark this email.** Come back to it whenever anxiety shows up uninvited.

Want the full version with 7 more techniques? The [Anxiety Management Guide](https://mspaultherapies.com/guide) covers everything from body-based to mind-based strategies.

You've got this.
**Aishani**

## Email 5: Let's Talk About the Stigma
**Send:** Day 9
**Subject:** "But therapy is for crazy people" — let's talk about this
**Preview text:** What I hear from Indian families (and what I say back)

---

Hi,

I grew up hearing it too.

"Just pray about it."
"Why do you need a stranger to solve your problems?"
"Log kya kahenge?" (What will people say?)
"We didn't have therapy in our day and we turned out fine."

These aren't bad intentions. They come from a culture that values family, resilience, and privacy. I respect that deeply.

But here's what I've seen in my practice:

**The same people who say "we don't need therapy" are often the ones who need it most.** Because they've been carrying everything alone for decades — and calling it strength.

Let me reframe something:

| Old belief | New perspective |
|-----------|---------------|
| "Therapy means something is wrong with me" | Therapy means I'm investing in myself |
| "I should handle it within the family" | Some things need a neutral, trained perspective |
| "It's just a phase" | If a "phase" lasts months, it deserves attention |
| "I'll deal with it when it gets worse" | Prevention is easier than crisis management |

**You don't need permission to take care of your mental health.** Not from your parents, not from your partner, not from society.

If you're reading this and thinking "maybe..." — trust that feeling.

With respect for where you come from and hope for where you're going,
**Aishani**

## Email 6: What Actually Happens in Therapy?
**Send:** Day 11
**Subject:** What actually happens in a therapy session?
**Preview text:** Spoiler: you don't have to lie on a couch

---

Hi,

The #1 reason people avoid therapy isn't cost or stigma — it's **not knowing what to expect.**

So let me walk you through a typical first session with me:

**Before we meet:**
You'll fill out a brief intake form. Nothing scary — just basic information so I can prepare.

**The first 5 minutes:**
We settle in. I ask how you're feeling about being here. It's normal to be nervous. I won't rush you.

**The next 30 minutes:**
I'll ask about what brought you here, your background, and what you're hoping to get from therapy. You share at your own pace — there's no pressure to reveal everything on day one.

**The last 10 minutes:**
We'll discuss what we noticed together, whether this feels like a good fit, and what the next steps might look like. If I'm not the right therapist for you, I'll honestly tell you and refer you to someone who might be better.

**What I WON'T do:**
- ❌ Judge you
- ❌ Tell you what to do
- ❌ Share anything with your family (confidentiality is absolute)
- ❌ Make you feel "crazy" for being here
- ❌ Rush the process

**What therapy actually feels like:**
Most clients describe it as "talking to someone who really, really listens." Someone who asks questions that make you think. Someone who holds space for the parts of you that you don't show anyone else.
