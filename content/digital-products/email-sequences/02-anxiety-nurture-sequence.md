## Email 1: The Anxiety Loop
**Subject:** The anxiety loop (and how to break it)
**Preview:** Understanding why anxiety keeps coming back

---

Hi,

If anxiety feels like a hamster wheel — the same worries, the same dread, the same "what ifs" on repeat — you're not broken. You're stuck in a loop.

Here's how the loop works:

**1. A trigger happens** (or you imagine one)
**2. Your brain sounds the alarm** ("DANGER!")
**3. Your body reacts** (racing heart, tight chest, stomach knots)
**4. You avoid the thing** (cancel plans, procrastinate, scroll your phone)
**5. You feel temporary relief** ("Phew, I don't have to deal with that")
**6. Your brain learns: avoidance = safety**
**7. Next time, the anxiety is STRONGER**

The cruel trick? The very thing that gives you short-term relief (avoidance) makes the long-term anxiety worse.

**The first step to breaking the loop is simply recognising it.**

This week, notice: When I feel anxious, what do I avoid? What's the short-term relief? And does the anxiety come back stronger later?

You don't need to change anything yet. Just observe.

Over the next few emails, I'll share specific tools for each part of this loop — body, mind, and behaviour.

Aware is halfway there.
**Aishani**

## Email 2: Your Body Is Talking
**Subject:** Your body is talking — are you listening?
**Preview:** 3 body-based anxiety techniques that work in under 2 minutes

---

Hi,

Anxiety isn't just in your head. It lives in your body:
- The tight jaw you don't notice until bedtime
- The shallow breathing you've been doing all day
- The tension in your shoulders that's been there for weeks

Your nervous system is stuck in "fight or flight." Here are 3 ways to switch it off:

**1. The Physiological Sigh (Stanford Research)**
- Double inhale through your nose (sniff-sniff)
- Long slow exhale through your mouth
- Do this ONCE. One cycle is enough to measurably reduce stress hormones.
- Works in: 10 seconds

**2. Cold Water Reset**
- Splash cold water on your face, wrists, and the back of your neck
- This triggers the "dive reflex" — your heart rate drops, blood pressure lowers
- Works in: 30 seconds

**3. Butterfly Hug (EMDR-derived)**
- Cross your arms over your chest, hands on opposite shoulders
- Alternately tap left-right, left-right, slowly
- Continue for 1–2 minutes while breathing deeply
- Works in: 2 minutes

**My recommendation:** Try all three this week and notice which one your body responds to best. That becomes your go-to.

Your body already knows how to calm down. You just need to give it the signal.

More tomorrow on what to do with the thoughts.
**Aishani**

## Email 3: The Thought That Keeps You Stuck
**Subject:** The thought that keeps you stuck
**Preview:** How to stop believing everything your anxious mind tells you

---

Hi,

Your anxious mind is a very convincing liar.

It says things like:
- "Something terrible is going to happen"
- "Everyone is judging you"
- "You're not good enough"
- "What if you fail?"

And it says them with such authority that you believe them. But here's the thing: **thoughts are not facts.**

**The 3-Question Challenge:**

Next time an anxious thought shows up, ask:

**1. "Is this a fact or a worry?"**
A fact has evidence. A worry is a prediction. Most anxiety is about things that haven't happened yet.

**2. "What would I tell my best friend?"**
If your friend said "I'm going to fail and everyone will think I'm stupid," would you agree? Or would you say "That's not true. You're prepared. One outcome doesn't define you."

**3. "What's the most LIKELY outcome?"**
Not the worst case. Not the best case. The most realistic, probable outcome. Usually, it's much more boring than your anxiety predicts.

**Example:**
- Anxious thought: "I'll mess up the presentation and get fired"
- Fact or worry? Worry.
- What would I tell a friend? "You've done presentations before. You know your stuff."
- Most likely outcome? "I'll be nervous but get through it. Nobody will remember in a week."

This is called cognitive restructuring — the core of CBT. It gets easier with practice.

I've created a [CBT Thought Record worksheet](https://mspaultherapies.com/guide) you can print and use daily. It walks you through this process step by step.

You don't have to believe every thought that shows up.

## Email 4: The Surprising Thing About Avoidance
**Subject:** The surprising thing about avoidance
**Preview:** Why running from anxiety makes it chase you harder

---

Hi,

There's a paradox at the heart of anxiety:

**The more you avoid what makes you anxious, the more anxious you become.**

It seems logical to avoid: "If that situation makes me anxious, I'll just... not do it." But every time you avoid, your brain records: "That was dangerous. Good thing we escaped."

The problem? It WASN'T dangerous. It was just uncomfortable.

**Avoidance teaches your brain that discomfort = danger.**

And then the world gets smaller:
- Skip one party → avoid all social events
- Leave one meeting early → dread all meetings
- Avoid one conversation → withdraw from relationships

**The antidote: Gradual exposure.**

Not "face your fears" in a dramatic way. More like:

**Step 1:** Make a list of things you avoid (be specific)
**Step 2:** Rank them from least scary to most scary
**Step 3:** Start with the LEAST scary one
**Step 4:** Do it. Let the anxiety come. Don't fight it. Notice it.
**Step 5:** Stay in the situation until the anxiety naturally drops (it always does — usually within 20–30 minutes)
**Step 6:** Repeat until that step feels manageable
**Step 7:** Move to the next one

**The key insight:** Anxiety peaks and then falls. EVERY time. If you stay long enough, your brain learns "Oh... this isn't actually dangerous."

This is the most evidence-based treatment for anxiety. And you can start small.

What's ONE thing you've been avoiding that you could try this week?

Rooting for you.
**Aishani**
