## Email 1: Self-Care Is Not What Instagram Told You
**Day 0 | Subject:** Self-care is not what Instagram told you
**Preview:** The real kind doesn't photograph well

---

Hi,

Self-care has been hijacked.

Instagram says it's face masks, scented candles, and "treating yourself." And sure, those things are nice. But they're not self-care — they're self-soothing. There's a difference.

**Self-soothing** = things that make you feel better in the moment
**Self-care** = things that make your life better over time

Real self-care often looks like:
- Going to bed on time instead of watching one more episode
- Having that difficult conversation you've been avoiding
- Saying no to plans when you need rest
- Setting a boundary with a family member
- Booking that doctor's appointment
- Logging off social media for the evening

**Real self-care isn't always comfortable. But it's always kind.**

This week, ask yourself: "Am I self-soothing or self-caring?" Both are valid. But if you're only doing one, the other area needs attention.

More practical strategies coming your way.
**Aishani**

## Email 2: The 4 Types of Rest
**Day 2 | Subject:** You're not lazy — you're resting wrong
**Preview:** There are 7 types of rest and sleep is only one

---

Hi,

"I slept 8 hours and I'm STILL exhausted."

Sound familiar? That's because sleep is only ONE type of rest. Dr. Saundra Dalton-Smith identified 7 types your body needs:

**1. Physical rest** — Sleep + passive rest (stretching, massage, lying down)
**2. Mental rest** — Breaks from cognitive overload (scheduling short breaks, brain dumps before bed)
**3. Emotional rest** — Space to express feelings honestly without performing "I'm fine"
**4. Social rest** — Time with people who energise you; time AWAY from people who drain you
**5. Sensory rest** — Breaks from screens, noise, bright lights, notifications
**6. Creative rest** — Exposure to beauty — nature, art, music — without producing anything
**7. Spiritual rest** — Connection to meaning, purpose, community, or something bigger than yourself

**Your rest audit:**

Which types are you getting? Which are you neglecting?

Rest isn't lazy. It's strategic.

## Email 3: The Minimum Viable Self-Care Day
**Day 4 | Subject:** What self-care looks like on your worst day
**Preview:** When you can barely function, do these 5 things

---

Hi,

Some days, even the idea of a "routine" feels impossible.

On those days, you don't need a morning routine, a workout, or a gratitude journal. You need the **Minimum Viable Self-Care Day:**

**1. Drink water.** Fill a bottle. Keep it next to you. Sip.

**2. Eat something.** It doesn't need to be healthy. Toast counts. Biscuits count. Fuel is fuel.

**3. Step outside for 60 seconds.** Not a walk. Not exercise. Just stand outside and feel the air. Look at the sky.

**4. Move your body for 30 seconds.** Stretch your arms overhead. Roll your neck. Shake your hands. That's it.

**5. Text one person.** "Hey, thinking of you." or "I'm having a tough day." You don't need to explain more.

**That's the entire list.**

On bad days, this IS success. This is self-care.

The voice that says "that's not enough" is the same voice that's keeping you stuck. Don't listen to it. Listen to the quieter voice that says "at least I tried."

Tomorrow might be easier. Today, this is enough.
**Aishani**

## Email 4: Boundaries ARE Self-Care
**Day 7 | Subject:** The self-care nobody talks about: saying no
**Preview:** Why boundaries are the most important self-care tool

---

Hi,

The most powerful self-care tool doesn't cost anything, takes seconds, and will transform your life:

**The word "no."**

I know. In Indian culture especially, saying no feels like rebellion. It feels disrespectful, selfish, unkind.

But here's the truth I've learned as a therapist: **every yes to something you don't want is a no to something you need.**

- Yes to staying late at work = No to dinner with family
- Yes to absorbing everyone's problems = No to your own peace
- Yes to the family event you dread = No to rest you desperately need

**Boundaries are not walls. They're gates.** They decide what comes in and what stays out.

**3 boundary phrases that work:**
1. "I can't take that on right now."
2. "Let me think about it and get back to you." (Buys you time — never decide under pressure)
3. "I appreciate you asking. This time I need to decline."

**Your challenge this week:** Say no to ONE thing you would normally say yes to reluctantly. Notice how it feels. The guilt is temporary. The relief is lasting.

I've created a complete [Healthy Boundaries Checklist](https://mspaultherapies.com/guide) with scripts for family, work, and friendships if you want to go deeper.

You matter too.
**Aishani**

## Email 5: The Self-Care Menu
**Day 9 | Subject:** Build your personal self-care menu (template inside)
**Preview:** Stop guessing. Have a plan ready.

---

Hi,

Here's why self-care fails: when you need it most, you're too depleted to think of what to do.

The fix? **Build your self-care menu BEFORE you need it.**

Think of it like a restaurant menu — organised by what you need and how much time/energy you have:

**When I have 5 minutes:**
- _______________
- _______________
- _______________

**When I have 30 minutes:**
- _______________
- _______________
- _______________

**When I have a full evening:**
- _______________
- _______________
- _______________

**When I need connection:**
- _______________
- _______________

**When I need solitude:**
- _______________
- _______________

**When I need to move my body:**
- _______________
- _______________

**When I need to feel comforted:**
- _______________
- _______________

**Fill this in right now** (yes, right now — while you're feeling okay enough to think clearly) and save it somewhere accessible. Phone notes, printed on your fridge, screenshot it — wherever you'll see it on a hard day.

When the hard day comes, you won't need to think. You'll just open the menu.

One more email coming your way with a final thought.

## Email 6: Permission to Rest
**Day 12 | Subject:** You have permission to rest
**Preview:** A reminder you might need today

---

Hi,

I want to end this series with something simple:

**You have permission to rest.**

Not because you've earned it.
Not because you've been productive.
Not because everyone else is taken care of.

You have permission to rest **because you are a human being, and human beings need rest.**

You don't need to justify it.
You don't need to feel guilty about it.
You don't need to "make up for it" tomorrow.

Rest is not the reward for getting everything done.
Rest is what makes everything else possible.
