## Key Content

### Where Does Low Self-Esteem Come From?

"Self-esteem isn't something you're born with or without. It's built — and sometimes damaged — by:

1. **Childhood experiences** — criticism, conditional love, comparison with siblings
2. **Cultural messaging** — 'Log kya kahenge', beauty standards, academic pressure
3. **Social comparison** — Instagram highlight reels vs your behind-the-scenes
4. **Relationship dynamics** — controlling partners, friendships that drain you
5. **Trauma** — any experience that made you feel you weren't enough

The good news? What was learned can be unlearned."

### The Inner Critic Audit

"Everyone has an inner critic. The problem isn't having one — it's believing everything it says.

Exercise: Write down your inner critic's top 5 phrases.

Common ones:
- 'You're not good enough'
- 'Everyone is doing better than you'
- 'You don't deserve this'
- 'You're going to fail'
- 'Nobody really likes you'

Now — would you say any of these to your best friend? To a child? Then why do you accept it from yourself?"

### Rewriting the Narrative

"For each critic phrase, we write a balanced counter-statement:

| Critic Says | Counter-Statement |
|------------|------------------|
| 'You're not good enough' | 'I'm a work in progress and that's enough' |
| 'Everyone is doing better' | 'I only see their highlights, not their struggles' |
| 'You'll fail' | 'I've survived every failure so far and learned from them' |

Key: The counter-statement doesn't need to be positive. It needs to be TRUE."

### The Self-Worth Resume

"Create a document with:

**5 things I'm good at** (skills, not appearance)
**5 hard things I've survived**
**5 compliments I've received** (write them down — we forget the good)
**3 values I live by**
**3 times I surprised myself**

Read this on bad days. This is evidence. Your critic has opinions. This resume has facts."
