## Key Content

### The 4 Parenting Styles

"Research identifies 4 styles (Baumrind):

1. **Authoritarian** — 'Because I said so.' High demand, low warmth.
   Impact: Obedient but anxious children, fear-based compliance, poor self-esteem.

2. **Permissive** — 'Whatever makes you happy.' Low demand, high warmth.
   Impact: Poor self-regulation, entitlement, difficulty with authority.

3. **Uninvolved** — Disengaged. Low demand, low warmth.
   Impact: Attachment issues, poor academic/social outcomes.

4. **Authoritative** — 'I hear you, AND here are the limits.' High demand, high warmth.
   Impact: Best outcomes — confident, resilient, emotionally intelligent children.

The goal is authoritative: warm AND firm. Not either/or."

### Emotional Coaching (The Gottman Method for Kids)

"When your child is upset, the instinct is to fix it. Resist.

**Step 1: Notice the emotion.** 'I can see you're really upset.'
**Step 2: Label it.** 'It sounds like you're feeling frustrated because...'
**Step 3: Validate.** 'That makes sense. I'd feel that way too.'
**Step 4: Problem-solve TOGETHER.** 'What do you think we could do about it?'

The magic: validation BEFORE solutions. When children feel heard, they calm down. When they calm down, they can think."

### The Repair Framework (After You've Yelled)

"Every parent yells. The research says it's not the rupture that matters — it's the REPAIR.

1. **Cool down first** (even 5 minutes)
2. **Go to your child.** Get on their level physically.
3. **Own it:** 'I shouldn't have yelled. That wasn't okay.'
4. **Explain:** 'I was feeling frustrated because ___.'
5. **Reconnect:** 'I love you even when I'm upset. Can we try again?'

This teaches children that:
- Adults make mistakes and take responsibility
- Conflict can be repaired
- Emotions are manageable
- They are loved unconditionally"

### Screen Time — A Realistic Approach

"Instead of rigid time limits, focus on:

**WHAT** they're watching (passive consumption vs creative/educational)
**WHEN** (not during meals, not before bed, not as a babysitter replacement)
**WITH WHOM** (co-viewing = connection; solo viewing = isolation)
**WHAT'S DISPLACED** (is screen time replacing sleep, play, or social time?)

The conversation matters more than the clock."

### Hard Conversations by Age

| Topic | Ages 5–8 | Ages 9–12 | Ages 13–17 |
|-------|----------|-----------|------------|
| Death/grief | 'Their body stopped working. It's okay to feel sad.' | 'Death is part of life. What questions do you have?' | Open, honest conversation. Let them process. |
| Divorce | 'Mama and Papa love you. We just live in different houses now.' | 'Our relationship changed. This is not your fault.' | 'You can feel angry. Let's talk about it.' |
| Mental health | 'Sometimes our feelings get too big. There are people who help.' | 'Mental health is like physical health. We all need help sometimes.' | 'Have you ever felt worried or sad in a way that doesn't go away? I'm here.' |
| Bullying | 'If someone makes you feel bad, tell me. I'll help.' | 'Bullying is about THEM, not you. What happened?' | 'I want to understand what's going on. No judgement.' |
